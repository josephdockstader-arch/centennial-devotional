Cohort One · Week Of October 12 · Six Talks

The Only Begotten

The Council and the Covenant

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

John 3:16

The talks

  1. Office Where scripture calls him the Only Begotten, and what the name claims. Work John 1:14 and 18, John 3:16-18, 1 John 4:9, and Moses 1:6 together, and open the week by telling us what kind of Son the word means.
  2. Restoration Abraham 3:22-28. The council before this world, the noble and great ones, and the two who answered "Here am I, send me." What the Prophet Joseph Smith restored that no other record on earth carries.
  3. Text Moses 4:1-4, set beside Abraham 3:27. Two proposals were made. Compare them line by line, especially "surely I will do it" against "thy will be done," and "give me thine honor" against "the glory be thine forever."
  4. World What ancient Israel understood about a life before this one, and what the early Christians did with it. Job 38:4-7, Jeremiah 1:5, John 9:2, Ephesians 1:4. Find out when and why the doctrine faded from Christian teaching.
  5. Witness Moses, in Moses 1:1-22. He is shown the works of God, told "thou art my son," and then confronted by Satan demanding worship. Take him through the questions on the facing page and tell us what he came to know standing on that mountain.
  6. Objection The charge that a premortal existence is an invention with no scriptural basis. Name it plainly and answer it from the text.

The title

The word behind "only begotten" in the Greek New Testament is monogenes, and it carries two ideas at once: a son who is genuinely a son, and a son who is the only one of his kind. John uses it five times, and every time he is pressing the same point, that the relationship between the Father and this Son is not like any other relationship in scripture. Israel is called God's son. Adam is called the son of God in Luke's genealogy. We are told we may become the sons and daughters of God. None of that is what John means here. He means a Son who was there before the world, who was sent rather than raised up, and who came because he was given. Notice that in John 3:16 the giving is what the love consists of. The Father's love for the world is measured by what the Son was sent to do, and the Son's willingness is measured by his answer in Abraham 3:27. This cohort is about that answer, made before any of us had a body, and about what it committed him to.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Abraham 3:22-28
  • Moses 4:1-4
  • John 3:16-18 and John 1:14-18
  • Revelation 12:7-11
  • Job 38:4-7

Questions for thought

  1. In Abraham 3:27 two beings answer the same call with almost the same words. What is different about the two answers, and how much of the difference is in the words themselves?
  2. Moses 4:1 records a proposal to redeem all mankind so that "one soul shall not be lost." Why would that plan be rejected? What does it cost that the accepted plan does not?
  3. Revelation 12:11 says they overcame him "by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony." The blood had not been shed yet. What does that tell you about how the covenant worked?
  4. Jeremiah is told "before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee." What would it change about how you read the rest of Jeremiah's life if you took that verse literally?
  5. Job 38 is God questioning Job. Why does he bring up the morning stars singing at the foundation of the earth in the middle of an argument about suffering?
  6. Everyone in this room was at that council. What difference should that make on a Tuesday?
  7. Find one thing a Christian writer from the first three centuries said about a life before this one. What happened to that idea?
  8. The Savior's willingness came before his birth, and it was tested again in the wilderness and again in the garden. Is willingness that has to be renewed still the same willingness?
  9. What is the difference between being chosen and being sent?
