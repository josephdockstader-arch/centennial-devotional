The Only Begotten

The Council and the Covenant

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

John 3:16

About this name

John describes the Father's love through the gift of his Only Begotten Son. Other scriptures call human beings children of God, but this title identifies Christ's unique relationship with the Father. The Book of Mormon and the Doctrine and Covenants call him the Only Begotten of the Father in the flesh (2 Nephi 25:12; D&C 93:11).

Abraham 3 and Moses 4 take us to the council before mortal life. The Son offers to do the Father's will; Lucifer seeks the Father's honor and the destruction of human agency. The difference concerns the kind of salvation being proposed and the relationship between God and his children. The Son's willingness in that council begins a work he will carry through mortality, Gethsemane, and the cross.

This program reads the title in a particular way. Begetting is meant in a begetting sense. The uniqueness is in the condition of the Father at the begetting: he fathers this Son as a glorified being, and no one else can say that. Adam had children, and Adam was not glorified when he had them.

Required reading for every speaker

  • Abraham 3:18–28; Moses 4:1–4.
  • John 1:1–18; 3:14–21; 17:1–5, 20–26.
  • 2 Nephi 2:11–30; 25:12–13; Alma 13:1–9.
  • D&C 29:36–45; 93:1–4, 11–17, 21–40, with 29–32 on intelligence, truth, and agency.
  • Revelation 12:7–11; Job 38:1–11; Jeremiah 1:4–10.
  • Joseph Smith, "The Great Plan of Salvation," chapter 17.
  • John Taylor, *Mediation and Atonement*, chapter XI, on the council and agency.
  • Luke 22:39–46; Hebrews 10:1–10; Moses 1:39.
  • John Taylor, "Agency and Accountability," chapter 15.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Compare the two offers in Abraham 3:27 with Moses 4:1–4. What does each speaker offer, and what does each ask for? What would be lost if salvation meant that no one could choose otherwise?
  2. D&C 93 connects agency with intelligence and truth. How does that account deepen the objection to Lucifer's proposal? Is agency merely permission to select between options?
  3. In John 17, what does Jesus ask the Father to give his disciples that he himself already shares with the Father? How does this help distinguish Christ's sonship from ours while explaining our relationship to him?
  4. Read Alma 13 alongside Abraham 3 and Jeremiah 1. What relationship do these passages establish between preparation before birth and responsibility in mortality? What would justify, or challenge, the conclusion that a person's future is fixed?
  5. John Taylor's chapter XI interprets the disagreement in the council. Which parts of his explanation follow directly from Moses and Abraham, and where does he develop an argument of his own? Which part deserves further examination?
  6. Revelation 12 describes victory through the Lamb's blood. Compare that passage with Alma 13:1–9. How do these texts speak of redemption before the events of Christ's mortal ministry?
  7. Why does God's answer to Job begin with creation? Does knowing that life extends beyond mortality resolve Job's complaint, change it, or leave it standing in a different form?
  8. Compare Christ's willingness in Moses 4:2 with his prayer in Luke 22:39–46, included as a research reading below. What does the later prayer add to the meaning of the earlier offer?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. What difference does believing you lived before your birth make to the way you understand yourself? Could that belief also change how you regard someone you find difficult to respect?
  2. The program teaches that you were at that council and that you answered. What would someone watching an ordinary week of your life conclude about whether you believe it?
