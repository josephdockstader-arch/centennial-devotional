Cohort Four · Week Of November 9 · Six Talks

The Hope of Israel

The Silence Between

O the hope of Israel, the saviour thereof in time of trouble, why shouldest thou be as a stranger in the land, and as a wayfaring man that turneth aside to tarry for a night?

Jeremiah 14:8

The talks

  1. Office Where he is called the hope of Israel. Jeremiah 14:8 and 17:13, and then Acts 28:20, where Paul stands in chains in Rome and says it is for the hope of Israel that he is bound. Tell us what it means for a person to be a nation's hope.
  2. Restoration What was happening on this continent while Judea went quiet. The Nephite record does not stop for four hundred years. Show us what they were being told, and what it means that the Lord kept speaking somewhere.
  3. Text Malachi 3 and 4, the last words of the Old Testament. Read what Israel was left holding, and what it told them to watch for.
  4. World The four hundred years. Alexander, the Greeks, the desecration of the temple, the Maccabean revolt, and then Rome. Find out who was governing Judea in the year he was born and how that man got the job.
  5. Witness Daniel, whose prophecy in Daniel 9:24-27 counts the years to the Messiah. He was in exile in a foreign court. Take him through the questions on the facing page.
  6. Objection Why would God go silent for four hundred years on a covenant people? Answer it. Amos 8:11-12 is a place to start, and so is our own account of an apostasy.

The title

This is the only cohort in the year when he is not on the page. That is the point of it. Between the last verse of Malachi and the first chapter of Luke there are four centuries, and in that stretch Israel is conquered by Greeks, briefly free under the Maccabees, and then conquered again by Rome. No prophet speaks. The title comes from Jeremiah, who twice calls the Lord the hope of Israel, and it is worth noticing that Jeremiah says it during a drought, in a complaint. He is not celebrating. He is asking why the hope of Israel is acting like a traveler who stops for one night and moves on. That is what four hundred years of waiting feels like from the inside. When Paul is finally brought to Rome as a prisoner he tells the Jewish leaders there that he wears the chain for the hope of Israel, and he means a person, not a feeling. Everything this cohort covers happened in the dark, to people who did not know how long the dark would last. Your job this week is to make the school feel that length.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Malachi 3 and 4
  • Jeremiah 14:7-9 and 17:13
  • Daniel 9:20-27
  • Amos 8:11-12
  • Acts 28:17-22

Questions for thought

  1. Jeremiah calls him the hope of Israel in the middle of a complaint. What does that tell you about what hope is?
  2. Malachi ends with a promise about Elijah and a warning about the earth being smitten with a curse. Why is that the last thing Israel is given before the silence?
  3. The Maccabees won. Judea was independent for about eighty years. Why did that not turn out to be the deliverance they were waiting for?
  4. Find out how Herod became king of the Jews and who gave him the title. What would you have thought of him if you had lived there?
  5. Amos describes a famine of hearing the words of the Lord. What would that famine look like in a person's life rather than a nation's?
  6. Daniel prophesied on a timeline. Read Daniel 9:24-27 and find out how people have counted it. Why would God give a number instead of a sign?
  7. Four hundred years is roughly the distance between us and the first English settlements in America. Does knowing that change how long the silence feels?
  8. What kept faithful families faithful with no prophet to go to? Find a specific practice, not a general answer.
  9. Have you ever had to keep a commitment during a stretch when you got no confirmation at all?
