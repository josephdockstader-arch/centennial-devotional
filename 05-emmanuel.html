Emmanuel

Be It Unto Me

Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.

Matthew 1:23

About this name

Matthew translates Emmanuel as "God with us." The name identifies the divine significance of the child entering Mary and Joseph's household. When scripture says God it is naming an office, and the office here is the Father's. The child is the Son. He comes representing that office and speaking for the Father, and he is not the Father. Isaiah 8:8 addresses the land of Judah as Immanuel, so the name does not work as a simple identification of one person with God. Matthew's claim is that the presence among us carries the Father's own authority.

Luke's account follows Mary through the announcement, her visit to Elisabeth, and her song about God's mercy and power. Matthew follows Joseph's response to the pregnancy and his decision to take Mary as his wife. Nephi's vision places the mother and child at the center of a question about God's condescension. Together these accounts make the Incarnation a subject involving bodies, families, promises, and decisions with immediate consequences.

Required reading for every speaker

  • Luke 1:5–56; Matthew 1:18–25.
  • Isaiah 7:1–16; 9:6–7.
  • 1 Nephi 11:13–23; Mosiah 3:5–8; Alma 7:9–13.
  • John 1:1–18; Philippians 2:5–11; Hebrews 2:14–18.
  • D&C 93:1–17; Moses 1:1–6.
  • John Taylor, Mediation and Atonement, chapter XVIII, on Christ's sonship.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. What does "God with us" claim about Jesus? Compare Matthew's language with John 1, Mosiah 3, and D&C 93. How do these passages describe his divinity and his relationship with the Father?
  2. Reconstruct Ahaz's problem from Isaiah 7:1–16. What would a sign mean in that crisis? How does Matthew use the promise when he tells the story of Jesus's birth?
  3. Compare Mary's question in Luke 1:34 with Zacharias's question in Luke 1:18. What reasons does the narrative give for Gabriel's different responses? Is asking how a promise will be fulfilled necessarily an expression of disbelief?
  4. Research betrothal and divorce in the setting of Matthew 1. What options does Matthew show Joseph considering, and what does his initial choice reveal about his understanding of righteousness?
  5. Mary sings about rulers, the lowly, the hungry, and the rich. How does her song interpret the coming of Christ? What would be missing from an account of his birth concerned only with private happiness?
  6. Why might seeing Mary and the child help Nephi understand the condescension of God? Compare the vision with the actions attributed to Christ in Philippians 2.
  7. Taylor distinguishes Christ's position from that of other sons of God. Compare his reasoning with D&C 93:11–17, 21–23. What does that distinction explain, and what relationship between Christ and humanity remains?
  8. Hebrews 2 connects Christ's participation in flesh and blood with his ability to help. What does sharing the human condition make possible in the work of redemption?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. Have you accepted a responsibility before knowing everything it would require? What helped you continue as you learned more? How does that experience affect what you hear in Mary's answer?
  2. If God is with us, what should be different about how a person behaves when nobody is watching? Is it different for you?

Additional research readings

  • 1 Samuel 2:1–10 beside Mary's song; Matthew 28:16–20 beside Emmanuel.
  • Joseph Smith, "Jesus Christ, the Divine Redeemer of the World," chapter 3.
