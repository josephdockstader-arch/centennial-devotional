Cohort Five · Week Of November 30 · Six Talks

The Lord

Prepare Ye the Way

The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make straight in the desert a highway for our God.

Isaiah 40:3

The talks

  1. Office Isaiah 40:3 and Malachi 3:1, "the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple." All four Gospel writers quote the Isaiah passage about John. Tell us what the title carries, and why the forerunner's whole message is built on that one word.
  2. Restoration The forerunner as an office rather than a single man. D&C 27:7, D&C 84:26-28, and D&C 77:9 and 14. John was an Elias. Show us what that word does and why the article matters.
  3. Text Luke 1:5-25 and 57-79. Zacharias in the temple, struck dumb for nine months, and then the Benedictus, the first thing he says when his mouth is opened.
  4. World The temple priesthood. Find out how the courses worked, how many priests there were, how the lot was cast to burn incense, and why a man in Zacharias's position would likely never have that duty again in his life.
  5. Witness Elisabeth. Barren, past childbearing, and the first person on record to greet Mary as the mother of her Lord. Take her through the questions on the facing page.
  6. Objection Later, from prison, John sends to ask "art thou he that should come, or look we for another?" Luke 7:18-28. The greatest prophet born of women asks whether he was right. Name that plainly and answer it.

The title

Every one of the four Gospel writers reaches for the same verse when he introduces John the Baptist, and the verse is Isaiah 40:3. That is unusual. The four of them agree on very little in terms of what to include, but all four decided the reader had to have this line first: prepare ye the way of the Lord. In Isaiah the picture is a road crew going out ahead of a king, filling in valleys and knocking down hills so the road is straight enough for him to travel. That is the whole of John's job description, and he understood it that way. He does not say he is bringing anything. He says he is clearing something. Malachi adds the piece that gives the title its weight, that the Lord whom ye seek shall suddenly come to his temple. Suddenly is the word to sit with. Israel had been waiting four hundred years, and when it finally happened it happened fast and most people missed it. This cohort covers the man whose entire life was spent getting a road ready for someone else to walk on.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Luke 1:5-25 and 57-80
  • Isaiah 40:1-5 and Malachi 3:1-4
  • Matthew 3:1-12
  • Luke 7:18-28
  • D&C 84:26-28

Questions for thought

  1. Zacharias asks Gabriel "whereby shall I know this?" Mary later asks "how shall this be?" One is struck dumb and one is not. What is different about the two questions?
  2. Zacharias could not speak for nine months. What do you think he did with that time, and why does Luke bother to tell us?
  3. John baptized in the wilderness rather than at the temple, twenty miles from the priesthood he was born into. Why do you think he set up out there?
  4. Find out what John ate and wore and what that would have signaled to a Jew who knew the scriptures.
  5. John says he must decrease. Is that humility, or is it a job description?
  6. In Luke 7 John sends from prison to ask if he was right about who Jesus was. Does that shake your confidence in John, or make you trust him more?
  7. Jesus answers John's question by listing things that were happening rather than saying yes. Why answer that way?
  8. What does it take to prepare a way for someone when you will not be traveling it yourself?
  9. Who prepared the way for you, and did they get to see the result?
