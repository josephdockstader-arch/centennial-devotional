The Prince of Peace

Unto You Is Born

For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.

Isaiah 9:6

The title

Isaiah 9:6 is one verse with five names in it, and they build. Wonderful, Counsellor, the mighty God, the everlasting Father, and then the Prince of Peace. Read the verse just before it and you find out why peace is the one it lands on. Isaiah has been describing a battle, boots and garments rolled in blood, and then the tone turns and a child is born. The Hebrew word behind peace is shalom, and it is bigger than the absence of fighting. It means a thing made whole, put back the way it was supposed to be. That is what a prince of peace is for. Notice also that the government shall be upon his shoulder, which is a strange thing to say about an infant, and Isaiah says it in the same breath. The child and the king are the same person, and Israel was given that sentence seven hundred years before anyone could act on it. This week we tell the story of the night it happened, and the two very different sets of people who were told about it first.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Luke 2:1-38
  • Matthew 1:18-25 and 2:1-23
  • Isaiah 9:2-7
  • 3 Nephi 1:4-21
  • Galatians 4:4-7

Questions for thought

  1. Luke tells shepherds. Matthew tells foreign scholars. If you only had one of the two accounts, what would you conclude about who this child came for?
  2. Shepherds were not respected and could not testify in court. Why send angels to them first?
  3. Find out how long the walk from Nazareth to Bethlehem is and what the terrain does. How many days, and in what condition?
  4. In 3 Nephi 1 believers were going to be killed if the sign did not appear on a certain day. What does that story add to Luke 2 that Luke 2 does not carry?
  5. Simeon holds the baby, blesses God, and then tells the mother a sword will pierce her soul. Why say that to her at that moment?
  6. Herod ordered children killed in Bethlehem. Find out roughly how many that would have been in a town that size, and why history outside the Bible may not have recorded it.
  7. Galatians says he was made under the law in order to redeem those under the law. Why did he have to be inside a thing to buy it back?
  8. Two genealogies, two different lines. Find out the main explanations that have been offered. Which do you find strongest, and what does it cost you?
  9. Everyone in this school has heard Luke 2 read aloud every December. What is in it that you have never noticed?
