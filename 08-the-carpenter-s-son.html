The Carpenter's Son

The Hidden Years

Is not this the carpenter's son? is not his mother called Mary? and his brethren, James, and Joses, and Simon, and Judas?

Matthew 13:55

About this name

The people of Nazareth identify Jesus through his family and trade. Matthew calls him the carpenter's son; Mark records the question, "Is not this the carpenter?" The neighbors' knowledge of his household becomes part of their objection to his authority.

Luke describes Jesus growing in wisdom and stature and returning from Jerusalem to live under his parents' authority. D&C 93 says he received grace for grace. Those statements make his development important even though the Gospels preserve few events from these years. The title invites us to consider the relationship between the life people could observe in Nazareth and the ministry that later astonished them.

Required reading for every speaker

  • Luke 2:39–52; Matthew 13:53–58; Mark 6:1–6.
  • John 7:1–18; Hebrews 2:10–18; 4:14–16; 5:5–9.
  • D&C 93:11–20; 88:118–126.
  • Mosiah 3:5–8; Alma 7:11–13; Moses 6:55–59.
  • Brigham Young, "Thrift, Industry, and Self-Reliance," chapter 31.
  • D&C 58:26–29; 130:18–19; Luke 16:10–12.
  • Brigham Young, "Learning by Study and by Faith," chapter 27.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Luke places Jesus's statement about his Father's business beside his return to parental authority. What relationship between calling, family obligation, and preparation does this sequence suggest?
  2. Compare "increased in wisdom" in Luke 2 with "grace for grace" in D&C 93. What does it mean for the Son of God to learn? How does Hebrews 5 contribute to the question?
  3. Research the word translated "carpenter" and the work available to craftspeople in first-century Galilee. How might the responsibilities of such work help you understand Jesus's participation in human life?
  4. In Matthew 13 and Mark 6, which facts about Jesus do his neighbors cite? Why do those facts become objections? When can familiarity help us recognize someone's gifts, and when can it prevent us?
  5. Compare Jesus's brothers' advice in John 7 with his response. What assumptions about recognition and success separate them? Have you seen similar assumptions affect how people judge a calling or vocation?
  6. Brigham Young gives ordinary labor religious importance. How does his treatment of work compare with the accounts of Jesus's growth? What could make work spiritually formative, and what would keep it from being so?
  7. Hebrews says Christ can help people who are tempted because he has suffered temptation. How does this affect what you believe he understands about fatigue, responsibility, and the effort to do what is right?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. Think of a responsibility you currently consider preparation for something more important. What value might it have already? Which of this week's readings most challenges or supports your answer?
  2. Most of his life is unrecorded, and he spent it working. What part of your own daily work do you treat as time to be got through?
