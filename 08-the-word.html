Cohort Eight · Week Of January 4 · Six Talks

The Word

That Ye Might Believe

And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

John 1:14

The talks

  1. Office John 1:1-14. Find out what logos meant to a Greek reader and what it meant to a Jewish one, and tell us why John chose a word that would land on both.
  2. Restoration The Joseph Smith Translation. What it is, how the Prophet made it, and what it is not. Bring us three specific places where it changes the sense, and start with JST John 1.
  3. Text John 20:30-31 and John 21:25. John tells us plainly that he selected, and that he left out more than he put in. Show us what he says his purpose was, and what that means for how we read him.
  4. World How a book was made in the first century. How long the accounts were carried by mouth before anyone wrote them down, what papyrus was, how copies were produced, and roughly how many early manuscripts survive.
  5. Witness Luke, who tells us in Luke 1:1-4 that he was not there, that others had already written accounts, and that he investigated and set his own in order. Take him through the questions on the facing page.
  6. Objection That the four accounts contradict each other. Do not soften it. Find three real discrepancies, say what they are, and then answer.

The title

John opens his account differently from everyone else. Matthew starts with a genealogy, Mark with the Baptist, Luke with a letter to a man named Theophilus. John starts before the world. And the name he uses is logos, which the King James translators rendered as Word. It was a shrewd choice on John's part, because logos meant one thing to Greek philosophers, the reason or ordering principle behind everything, and something else to Jewish readers, for whom the word of the Lord was how God acted and created and spoke through prophets. John picked a term that both halves of his audience already carried, and then he did something neither of them expected with it: he said it became flesh and moved into the neighborhood. This cohort is about the records themselves, four men writing four accounts of one life, and about what it means that we know him through documents. You will not get through this year without an opinion on these books. Form it this week, on evidence.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • John 1:1-18
  • Luke 1:1-4
  • John 20:30-31 and 21:24-25
  • Joseph Smith History 1:11-20
  • 1 Nephi 13:23-29

Questions for thought

  1. Luke says many had already written accounts before he did. What happened to those?
  2. John says the world could not contain the books that would be written if everything were recorded. Why tell the reader what you left out?
  3. Matthew, Mark, and Luke cover much of the same ground in similar order. John covers different material almost entirely. Why do you think that is?
  4. Find out roughly how many years passed between the crucifixion and the earliest written Gospel. What was happening to the eyewitnesses in that stretch?
  5. 1 Nephi 13 says plain and precious things were taken away. Find one place where the Joseph Smith Translation restores something and explain what difference it makes.
  6. If two witnesses to a car accident gave identical statements word for word, would you trust them more or less?
  7. John says he wrote so that ye might believe. Does knowing an author's purpose make you trust him more or watch him more closely?
  8. What is the difference between a biography and a testimony? Which are the Gospels, and does it matter?
  9. You will spend this whole year working from these four books. What do you need to settle about them before you can preach from them honestly?
