The Beloved Son

The Jordan and the Wilderness

And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

Matthew 3:17

About this name

At Jesus's baptism, the Father identifies him as his beloved Son. The voice at the Jordan and on the mount is the Father, speaking in the office of God. Nothing in the title requires the speaker and the Son to be one person, and this program does not read it that way. Matthew then takes him into the wilderness, where the adversary twice begins with a challenge to that identity. Jesus answers through passages from Deuteronomy about Israel's dependence on God, its worship, and its testing of the Lord.

The temptations involve hunger, protection, and dominion. Each offers a way to act on a real power or need. Studying the offers separately helps explain what Jesus refuses and why. His baptism also presents a question: why does the sinless Son submit to an ordinance associated with repentance? Nephi's explanation and Heber C. Kimball's discussion of Christ's example give the group material for considering obedience, authority, and the meaning of following him.

Required reading for every speaker

  • Matthew 3:13–4:11; Luke 4:1–14; John 1:29–34.
  • Deuteronomy 6:10–19; 8:1–5; Exodus 17:1–7.
  • 2 Nephi 31:4–21; Hebrews 4:14–16; 5:5–9.
  • Moses 1:12–22; D&C 20:22–24; 93:11–17.
  • Heber C. Kimball, "The Power of the Gospel," December 17, 1854, Journal of Discourses 2:233–235.
  • Psalm 91; James 1:12–18; D&C 121:34–46.
  • Joseph Smith, "The Mission of John the Baptist," chapter 6.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Compare the Father's declaration with the adversary's first words. What would Jesus be accepting if he agreed to prove his identity on the adversary's terms? Compare his response with Moses's in Moses 1.
  2. What is wrong with each proposed action: making bread, leaping from the temple, and accepting dominion through worship? Are the three temptations wrong for the same reason?
  3. Read each of Jesus's answers in its Deuteronomy setting. How does Israel's history help explain his choices? What does his use of scripture show beyond an ability to recall verses?
  4. The adversary also quotes scripture. What makes his use of Psalm 91 different from Jesus's use of Deuteronomy? How could a true passage be used to justify a wrong action?
  5. Nephi asks why the Lamb of God needed baptism. What answer does he develop? Compare it with Heber C. Kimball's use of Jesus's baptism in his sermon. What does baptism commit a follower of Christ to do?
  6. Hebrews describes a high priest who knows temptation without yielding to sin. Would never yielding mean that he understands temptation less? What experiences or arguments might support either answer?
  7. What difference should the Father's love make when a person is tempted to measure worth by recognition, success, or control? How does Jesus's experience help you think about that pressure?
  8. Identify a situation in which meeting a legitimate need could involve an unacceptable means. Which of the wilderness encounters gives you the most useful way to examine it?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. The Father told him who he was before the wilderness began. Whose approval are you working for, and what do you do to get it?
