Cohort Nine · Date to be Posted

The Beloved Son

The Jordan and the Wilderness

And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

Matthew 3:17

The title

Everything in this cohort turns on one word, and the word is Son. The Father says it out loud at the Jordan, in public, before the ministry has begun: this is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. And then Matthew takes him straight from the river into the desert, and the first thing the adversary says to him is if thou be the Son of God. Twice. The declaration is barely finished before it is attacked. That sequence is not an accident of arrangement, and Matthew clearly wants us to see it, because he puts the two scenes back to back with almost nothing in between. Notice also what he is well pleased with. Nothing has happened yet. No sermon, no miracle, no cross. The approval comes first and the work comes after, which is the reverse of how most of us assume it goes. This week covers the naming and the testing of the name, and they belong to the same story.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Matthew 3:13-17 and 4:1-11
  • John 1:19-37
  • 2 Nephi 31:4-13
  • Luke 4:1-14
  • Hebrews 4:14-16

Questions for thought

  1. The Father says he is well pleased before the ministry starts. What does that do to the idea that approval is something you earn?
  2. Satan opens two of three temptations with "if thou be the Son of God." Why attack the thing that was just publicly confirmed?
  3. Look at what he is actually offered: bread, safety, and authority over the kingdoms of the world. Were any of those things he was not eventually going to receive?
  4. He answers all three temptations by quoting Deuteronomy. Why answer with scripture rather than with power?
  5. John says he must decrease and then, in John 1:29, calls him the Lamb of God. How did John know that word to use?
  6. Find out what the Judean wilderness is physically like. Forty days there without food, what does that actually do to a body?
  7. Matthew 3:15 says it was done to fulfil all righteousness. What does that phrase mean?
  8. Hebrews says he was tempted in all points like as we are, yet without sin. Does being tempted and not yielding mean it was easier for him, or harder?
  9. When has something you knew about yourself been challenged right after it was confirmed?
