Cohort Nine · Week Of January 11 · Six Talks

The Carpenter's Son

The Hidden Years

Is not this the carpenter's son? is not his mother called Mary? and his brethren, James, and Joses, and Simon, and Judas?

Matthew 13:55

The talks

  1. Office Matthew 13:54-58 and Mark 6:3. It was said as an insult, by people who had watched him grow up. Tell us what the title meant to them, what it means as a plain fact, and why the Gospel writers kept it in.
  2. Restoration D&C 93:11-17. He received not of the fulness at the first, but continued from grace to grace. Show us what that teaches about the years nobody recorded.
  3. Text Luke 2:40-52. The temple at twelve, three days lost, and "wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" Then Luke 2:52, one verse covering eighteen years.
  4. World Nazareth. Find out what a tekton actually did and what he worked with, how big the town was, that Sepphoris was being rebuilt four miles away, what synagogue schooling a boy got and when, and what a family's week looked like.
  5. Witness Mary, who kept all these sayings in her heart. Luke says it twice. Take her through the questions on the facing page, and pay attention to what she is doing during those eighteen silent years.
  6. Objection The infancy gospels that circulated later, full of miracle stories about his childhood, and the modern theories that he spent the missing years in India or Britain. Say why we do not accept them, on evidence.

The title

This is the only title in the year that was meant as a put-down. He comes home to Nazareth, teaches in the synagogue, and the people who have known him since he was a boy are offended. Is not this the carpenter's son? They are not asking for information. They are saying we know exactly who you are, we know your mother and your brothers, and you are not what you are claiming to be. Mark's version drops the son and just says is not this the carpenter, which means he had the trade himself. The Greek word is tekton, and it covers more than furniture. It means a builder who worked in wood and stone, the kind of man you hired for a roof beam or a door frame or a yoke. So for something like eighteen years, the Savior of the world had a trade, a toolbox, and customers. The Gospels tell us almost nothing about that stretch, and this cohort is about the little they do tell us and about what a serious student can responsibly reconstruct around it.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Luke 2:39-52
  • Matthew 13:53-58 and Mark 6:1-6
  • D&C 93:11-17
  • John 7:1-5
  • Hebrews 2:14-18 and 4:15

Questions for thought

  1. Luke 2:52 covers about eighteen years in one sentence. If you had been Luke, what would you have wanted to ask Mary that he did not record?
  2. He stayed behind in Jerusalem for three days and his parents did not notice at first. What does that tell you about how those caravans traveled?
  3. At twelve he says he must be about his Father's business, and then he goes home and is subject to his parents for another eighteen years. Why the wait?
  4. Find out what a tekton in a small Galilean town would have built in a normal week, and who would have paid him.
  5. His brothers did not believe on him, according to John 7:5. What would it be like to grow up in that house?
  6. D&C 93 says he continued from grace to grace and received not of the fulness at first. What does that suggest about how he spent those years?
  7. Hebrews says he was tempted in all points like as we are. How much of that would have happened during the years nobody wrote down?
  8. Why do you think God chose to leave those years out of the record entirely?
  9. Most of your life will be years nobody writes down. What is being built in them?
