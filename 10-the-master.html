The Master

Fishers of Men

Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am.

John 13:13

The title

The disciples called him Master, and in the King James text that word is doing the work of several Greek words, including rabbi and didaskalos, teacher. It is worth knowing how the arrangement normally worked. A young man in first century Judea who wanted to learn would go and find a rabbi and apply, in effect, to follow him. The student chose the teacher. What happens in Galilee runs the other way. He walks up to men who are working, men who had not applied and were not students, and he says follow me. That reversal is the whole shape of this cohort. Then, on the last night, in the upper room, with a towel around him and having just washed their feet, he says ye call me Master and Lord, and ye say well, for so I am. He confirms the title at the exact moment he has done the lowest job in the house. Keep both halves. The one who called them is also the one who knelt in front of them.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Luke 5:1-11
  • John 1:35-51
  • Matthew 4:18-22 and 10:1-10
  • Mark 3:13-19
  • D&C 18:26-36

Questions for thought

  1. In the normal arrangement the student picked the teacher. Here the teacher picks the students. Why does that difference matter?
  2. Peter's first words after the miracle are "depart from me; for I am a sinful man." Why would a miracle produce that reaction?
  3. Find out what Zebedee's fishing business was worth and what it meant that he had hired servants. What did James and John walk away from?
  4. Matthew was a tax collector, which meant he worked for Rome against his own people. Simon was called Zelotes. How did those two get through three years in the same group?
  5. Nathanael insults Nazareth to the face of a man from Nazareth, and is called an Israelite in whom is no guile. Why is bluntness praised here?
  6. He called twelve rather than eleven or twenty. Find out why the number twelve would have carried meaning for Israel.
  7. He knew what Judas would do when he called him. What do you make of that?
  8. "Fishers of men" is a working man's phrase, not a religious one. Why use it?
  9. What have you been asked to leave, and did you know at the time what it would cost?
