The Sower

The Parables by the Sea

He answered and said unto them, He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man.

Matthew 13:37

About this name

Matthew 13 begins with Jesus teaching a crowd from a boat and later follows him into a house, where his disciples ask for an explanation. In explaining the wheat and tares, he identifies the sower of good seed as the Son of man. The chapter's other parables explore reception, growth, value, and judgment through familiar activities and objects.

The disciples themselves ask why he uses parables. His answer makes hearing and understanding part of the subject. Joseph Smith's treatment of the chapter brings the parables into a discussion of the kingdom of God and its development. This gives the group two connected tasks: interpret the stories in Matthew and examine the uses Joseph makes of them.

Required reading for every speaker

  • Matthew 13:1–52; Mark 4:1–34; Luke 8:4–18.
  • Isaiah 6:1–13; Psalm 78:1–8.
  • Alma 12:9–11; 32:26–43.
  • D&C 86:1–7; 101:63–68.
  • JST Matthew 13:10–11, printed in Matthew 13:12, footnote a.
  • Joseph Smith, "Truths from the Savior's Parables in Matthew 13," chapter 25.
  • Jacob 5:1–15, 47–77; 1 Corinthians 2:6–16.
  • D&C 6:7; 11:21–22; 50:17–22.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Compare Matthew's explanation of parables with Mark 4, Isaiah 6, and JST Matthew 13:10–11. What relationship do these texts describe between a hearer's choices and the ability to understand? Why might a story reach someone whom a direct statement would not?
  2. Research sowing practices relevant to the first parable. Which details help explain the seed's different outcomes? How does Jesus's interpretation direct attention toward the hearers?
  3. Compare the seed in Matthew 13 with the seed in Alma 32. What is being planted in each passage, and what must the hearer do? How does the difference affect the way each passage can be applied?
  4. Why does the householder delay separating wheat and tares? Compare Matthew's explanation with D&C 86. What dangers arise when people claim they can already identify everyone who belongs on either side?
  5. The treasure finder and the merchant both sell everything. What does each recognize that makes selling everything reasonable? How would you distinguish this kind of commitment from enthusiasm that will soon pass?
  6. Choose one interpretation Joseph Smith offers in chapter 25. Explain how he connects the details of the parable to the kingdom of God. Which parts of Matthew help you follow that interpretation?
  7. Jesus ends by describing a householder bringing out treasures old and new. How might that image explain the work of a student or teacher who has come to understand these parables?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. Which obstacle in the parable of the soils do you recognize most clearly in your current life? What would tending that ground involve? How might another person help?
  2. Which of the four soils describes you at present? What is your evidence?
