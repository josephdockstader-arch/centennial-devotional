The Bread of Life

The Five Thousand

And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.

John 6:35

About this name

John 6 follows a crowd from a meal to a dispute about who Jesus is and what he offers. After the feeding, some want to make him king. When they find him again, he directs the conversation toward the food that endures to everlasting life. Their reference to manna opens a discussion about the Father, the bread from heaven, and Christ's gift of his flesh for the life of the world.

Many disciples leave after this teaching. Peter stays because he believes Jesus has the words of eternal life. The chapter asks readers to consider what they seek from Christ and how they respond when his teaching differs from their expectations. The sacrament passages provide another setting in which receiving bread becomes part of a covenant with him.

Required reading for every speaker

  • John 6; Matthew 14:13–33.
  • Exodus 16:1–35; Deuteronomy 8:1–10; Isaiah 55:1–3.
  • Mosiah 3:17–19; 3 Nephi 18:1–14, 28–32; 20:1–9.
  • Moroni 4–5; D&C 20:75–79; 27:1–4.
  • Brigham Young, "Faith in the Lord Jesus Christ," chapter 8.
  • Exodus 12:1–28; 2 Kings 4:42–44; John 4:5–42.
  • John Taylor, Mediation and Atonement, chapter XVI, on Passover and the Lord's Supper.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Trace the crowd's requests through John 6. What do they want from Jesus at each stage? At what point does the disagreement become a dispute about his identity?
  2. Compare manna in Exodus 16 with Jesus's discussion of it. What did manna accomplish, and what does Jesus promise beyond it? Why might daily dependence be part of both accounts?
  3. Why does Jesus withdraw when people want to make him king? How might accepting their proposal have shaped the meaning of his ministry?
  4. Read John 6:35–58 alongside the sacrament prayers and 3 Nephi 18. What connections do you find among believing, coming, eating, remembering, and abiding? What does each passage contribute to understanding participation in Christ's life?
  5. Research the Passover setting named in John 6:4 and the identification of barley bread in verse 9. Compare Exodus 12 and 2 Kings 4:42–44. Which connections help explain the crowd's response to the feeding?
  6. Many disciples leave, and Jesus asks whether the twelve will also go. What reasons does Peter give for remaining? How do those reasons compare with Brigham Young's account of faith?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. Have you ever come to value a teaching or responsibility that you initially resisted? What changed your understanding? How might that experience help you read the reactions in this chapter?
  2. What are you seeking when you participate in the sacrament? Which phrase in the prayers could help you connect the ordinance more closely with following Christ during the week?
  3. Many disciples left when the teaching became hard. Which of his teachings do you avoid thinking about?
