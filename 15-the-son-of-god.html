The Son of God

Who Say Ye That I Am

And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.

Matthew 16:16

Date to be Posted · Required reading · Questions

About this name

Peter identifies Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God. Jesus attributes Peter's knowledge to revelation from the Father. When Jesus then teaches that he must suffer and be killed, Peter objects and receives a sharp rebuke. His confession is real, but he has not yet accepted what Jesus says the Messiah's work entails.

At the Transfiguration, the disciples receive another witness of the Son and a command to hear him. In John 5 and 8, Jesus's descriptions of his relationship with the Father provoke conflict. These passages make the title a claim that affects authority, worship, and what discipleship demands. This is the clearest case for the office rule. God here is theos. If God names an office rather than a person, then Son of God is settled by asking which office is in view and who holds it at that point, and the relation the title names is between the Son and the office his Father holds. The cohort considers both the grounds for confessing Christ and the learning that must follow such a confession.

Required reading for every speaker

  • Matthew 16:13–28; 17:1–13; Luke 9:28–36.
  • John 5:16–30; 8:48–59; Exodus 3:1–15.
  • 2 Peter 1:16–21; Mosiah 15:1–9.
  • D&C 76:19–24; 110:1–10; Joseph Smith—History 1:14–20.
  • Joseph Smith, "Jesus Christ, the Divine Redeemer of the World," chapter 3.
  • John 10:22–38; D&C 93:1–23.
  • John Taylor, Mediation and Atonement, chapter XVIII.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Compare the reported public opinions about Jesus with Peter's confession. What does Peter claim that the other answers do not? Which passages in this week's readings develop that difference?
  2. Why can Peter recognize the Messiah and still reject the announced suffering? What expectation does Jesus challenge? Can you think of a belief whose implications you accepted only gradually?
  3. Research Caesarea Philippi and its religious and political history. What might that setting contribute to the question of Jesus's identity? Which parts of your interpretation depend on the setting, and which stand on the conversation itself?
  4. Luke says Moses and Elias speak with Jesus about his coming departure at Jerusalem. How does that detail connect the Transfiguration with the suffering announced in Matthew 16?
  5. Compare the Father's words at the Transfiguration with his words in Joseph Smith—History. What does the command to hear the Son require beyond recognizing who he is?
  6. In John 5, Jesus speaks of both dependence on the Father and authority to give life and judge. How do those claims belong together? How do Mosiah 15 and Joseph Smith's teachings help frame the relationship?
  7. Compare John 8:58 with Exodus 3. Why is Jesus's statement more provocative than a claim to be a respected teacher? What does the response of his listeners indicate about how they hear it?
  8. What relationship does 2 Peter 1 establish between eyewitness testimony and prophecy? Compare that with the testimony recorded in D&C 76. How can different kinds of witness contribute to faith?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. If someone asked who Jesus is and why you trust him, which experience, teaching, or passage would you reach for first? What does that answer suggest you still want to understand about him?
  2. Peter believed he was the Christ and still argued with what Christ said the work required. What do you believe about him whose consequences you have not accepted?
