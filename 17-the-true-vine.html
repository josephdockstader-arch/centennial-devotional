The True Vine

The Upper Room

I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.

John 15:5

A featured devotional — to be announced.

About this name

Israel's scriptures describe a vineyard that receives careful cultivation and a vine brought out of Egypt. In John 15, Jesus calls himself the true vine and identifies his disciples as branches. Their capacity to bear fruit depends on remaining in him. He develops the image through teachings about his words, prayer, obedience, friendship, and love.

These teachings belong to the final evening before his arrest. Jesus prepares the disciples for his departure and promises the Comforter. The featured devotional considers how their relationship with him can continue when they no longer share his daily physical presence, and how that relationship should become visible in their life together.

Suggested reading

This is a featured devotional, so no cohort is assigned and nothing here is required of anyone. It is for students who want to come to the talk already knowing the texts behind it.

  • John 13:1–17, 31–35; 14–17.
  • Isaiah 5:1–7; Psalm 80:8–19.
  • Galatians 5:16–26; Moroni 7:43–48.
  • 3 Nephi 18:15–25; D&C 50:13–24; 88:62–68.
  • Parley P. Pratt, Key to the Science of Theology (1855), chapter X, especially its concluding discussion of the gift of the Holy Spirit.
  • Jacob 5:47–77; D&C 130:1–3, 22–23.
  • Joseph Smith, "Charity, the Pure Love of Christ," chapter 37.

Questions for study

  1. Compare the vine and vineyard in Isaiah 5, Psalm 80, and John 15. Who acts, who receives care, and what result is expected in each passage? What does "true" add to Jesus's claim?
  2. What does John 15 identify as the means of abiding in Christ? How do his words, commandments, love, and the disciples' prayers belong together?
  3. The Father prunes fruitful branches. What does that suggest about the relationship between fruitfulness and difficulty? How would you decide whether applying this image to a particular hardship is helpful?
  4. Jesus calls the disciples friends and still gives them commandments. What kind of friendship does he describe? How does it differ from either unquestioning servitude or a relationship without obligations?
  5. Compare the promised work of the Comforter in John 14–16 with Parley P. Pratt's description of the Holy Spirit's effects. What changes would another person be able to observe? Which belong to inward experience?
  6. What kind of unity does Jesus ask for in John 17? What would need to change in a group for that prayer to shape its disagreements, loyalties, and treatment of outsiders?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. When have you found it difficult to feel connected to Christ? Which practice or promise in these chapters offers a way to continue the relationship? Why that one?
  2. Describe what your connection to Christ consisted of over the last seven days. Be specific about what you did.
