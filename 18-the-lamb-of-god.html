The Lamb of God

Gethsemane and the Cross

The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

John 1:29

About this name

John the Baptist identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world. Passover, sacrificial worship, and Isaiah's account of the suffering servant give the title several scriptural connections. Hebrews explains Christ's offering through the work of a high priest entering the holy place.

The Gospel accounts bring us to Jesus's prayer in Gethsemane, his arrest and trials, and his crucifixion and burial. Doctrine and Covenants 19 adds his own account of suffering; Alma 34 develops the need for an infinite and eternal sacrifice. This cohort studies how these witnesses explain the Atonement and the response it asks of those who receive its benefits.

The talk week will be confirmed in the schedule.

Required reading for every speaker

  • John 1:19–34, where the Baptist gives the name.
  • Luke 22:39–23:56; John 18–19; Matthew 26:36–46; 27:45–54.
  • Exodus 12:1–14; Leviticus 16:1–22; Isaiah 52:13–53:12.
  • Hebrews 4:14–5:10; 9:1–28.
  • Mosiah 3:5–11; Alma 7:11–13; 34:8–17; 3 Nephi 9:19–22.
  • D&C 19:15–24; Moses 5:4–9.
  • John Taylor, "The Infinite Atonement of Jesus Christ," chapter 5.
  • Psalm 22; Mark 14:32–15:47; 1 Peter 1:18–21; Hebrews 10:1–22.
  • Luke 2:7–20; Genesis 35:19–21; Micah 4:8, for the flocks at Bethlehem and the tower called Migdal Eder.
  • Mishnah Shekalim 7:4, on animals found between Jerusalem and Migdal Eder, and Targum Pseudo-Jonathan on Genesis 35:21. Both are later than the Gospels and are evidence about the setting, not about it.
  • John Taylor, Mediation and Atonement, chapters XIX–XXI.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Compare the lamb in Exodus 12, the servant in Isaiah 53, and the sacrifices in Leviticus 16. What does each help explain about Christ? Which differences pull against each other when these images are brought together?
  2. People say the shepherds near Bethlehem kept the lambs raised for temple sacrifice, and that newborn lambs were swaddled and laid in a manger. Where do those claims come from? Do they hold up? What would they add to John calling Jesus the Lamb of God?
  3. In Gethsemane, what does Jesus request and what does he commit himself to do? How can both parts of the prayer express obedience? What does this suggest about prayers offered during an unwanted responsibility?
  4. Luke describes an angel strengthening Jesus while the suffering continues. What distinction does this make between being helped and having a trial removed? How might that affect what someone asks for or recognizes as help?
  5. Read D&C 19 beside the Gospel accounts. What does Christ emphasize when he describes his own suffering, and what does he ask the hearer to do? How does the passage connect the Atonement with repentance?
  6. Compare Peter's use of a sword in John 18 with his later denials. What different pressures does he face? How does Jesus's conduct challenge Peter's understanding of loyalty and courage?
  7. Research Pilate's office and responsibilities using the Bible Dictionary and John 18–19. How do the arguments made before him appeal to political power? What explains his decision, and what leaves him responsible for it?
  8. What did the temple veil separate? Use Leviticus 16 and Hebrews 9 to interpret its tearing in Matthew 27. What claim about access to God does that interpretation make?
  9. How does Alma 34 explain the need for an infinite sacrifice? Compare its argument with John Taylor's. What can repentance, obedience, or personal suffering accomplish, and what does the reading say requires Christ?
  10. Compare the sacrifice required in 3 Nephi 9:19–22 with the sacrifices in the earlier readings. What would offering a broken heart and contrite spirit involve for someone who wants to change but is ashamed to begin?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. Which saying or action of Jesus in these accounts most affects your understanding of his character? What is it about that detail that affects your trust in him?
  2. 3 Nephi 9 asks for a broken heart and a contrite spirit. What would you have to admit for that to be honest in your case?
