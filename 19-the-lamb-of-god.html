Cohort Nineteen · Week Of March 22 · Eight Talks

The Lamb of God

Gethsemane and the Cross

The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

John 1:29

The talks

  1. Office John said it three years before it meant anything, standing at a river. Work John 1:29 with Isaiah 53:7, Exodus 12, and Revelation 5:6-12. Why a lamb, and what did the man who said it understand himself to be announcing?
  2. Case Luke was a physician and he is the only one who records sweat like great drops of blood. John is the only one who gives us "It is finished." Find out what each writer kept and what each left out, and show us what each was arguing.
  3. Epistle Hebrews 5:7-9. In the days of his flesh, with strong crying and tears, and though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered. The Gospels show us the suffering. Hebrews says what it accomplished in him.
  4. Restoration D&C 19:16-19. His own account of Gethsemane, given in our dispensation, in the first person. Which suffering caused myself, even God, the greatest of all, to tremble because of pain, and to bleed at every pore. Nothing in the Bible carries this.
  5. Text Luke 22:39-46. Find out what the Greek word agonia meant and where else it is used. Work the cup, the prayer repeated three times, and the angel that came to strengthen him.
  6. World What a Roman scourging was and what it did to a body. What crucifixion was designed to accomplish and how long it usually took. Then find out how the night trial before the Sanhedrin broke their own law, and who Pilate was and what his position depended on.
  7. Witness Peter in the courtyard. An hour earlier he had drawn a sword for him in the dark. Then a servant girl asked him a question by a fire. Take him through the questions on the facing page.
  8. Objection How can the suffering of an innocent man justly answer a guilty man's debt? That is the oldest objection there is to the atonement. State it in its strongest form and then answer it.

The title

John the Baptist says it twice, on two consecutive days, and it is the strangest thing anyone says in the first chapter of John. Behold the Lamb of God. He is standing at a river, three years before the cross, pointing at a man walking toward him, and he uses the language of sacrifice. Every Jew listening knew what lambs were for. You brought one to the temple, and it died in your place, and you went home. The Passover lamb was chosen without blemish, killed at evening, and not one of its bones was broken. What John adds is the phrase that takes it past anything the temple ever did: which taketh away the sin of the world. Not the sin of a household for a year. The world. This is the week we come to what all of it was for, and it falls in the same week the Christian world keeps Good Friday. Speak carefully. Every one of you is standing up in front of a school that has heard this story since primary. Your job is to make them hear it as though the outcome were still in doubt.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Luke 22:39-23:56
  • John 18 and 19
  • Isaiah 53
  • D&C 19:15-24
  • Hebrews 5:7-9 and 9:11-28

Questions for thought

  1. He prayed the same prayer three times. Find out what he asked for and what he did not ask for.
  2. Luke says an angel came and strengthened him. Strengthened him to do what? The suffering did not stop.
  3. D&C 19 gives his own words about Gethsemane. Read it beside Luke 22. What does it add that changes how you read the Gospel account?
  4. Find out what the Sanhedrin's own rules said about trials at night and about capital cases. How many of their rules did that trial break?
  5. Pilate says he finds no fault in him and then has him scourged and crucified. Find out what Pilate's political situation was and why he might have folded.
  6. Peter drew a sword in the garden and denied him in the courtyard within about an hour. What is the difference between the two moments?
  7. He asked for the cup to pass. Does asking make the obedience less, or more?
  8. Find out what the veil of the temple was and what it separated, and then read Matthew 27:51 again.
  9. If the atonement had been only the cross, and not the garden, what would be missing?
