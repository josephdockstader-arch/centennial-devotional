The Redeemer

Made Alive in Christ

For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth.

Job 19:25

About this name

In Ruth 4, redemption is a public transaction involving family obligations, property, and the future of Ruth and Naomi. Boaz accepts a responsibility that a nearer relative declines. The account gives substance to the language of redemption used elsewhere in scripture: someone acts on behalf of people who cannot secure the desired future alone.

Paul describes Christ's work through several relationships and actions. People are justified, reconciled, adopted, freed from the dominion of sin, and raised to new life. The Book of Mormon develops the relation among mercy, justice, repentance, and grace. The readings ask what Christ makes possible and what it means for a person to receive that redemption and live differently because of it.

Required reading for every speaker

  • Ruth 3:1–4:17; Leviticus 25:23–28, 47–55; Job 19:23–27.
  • Romans 5–6; 8; Ephesians 2:1–10; James 2:14–26.
  • Hebrews 9:11–15; 10:1–25; Philippians 2:5–13.
  • 2 Nephi 2:6–10; 9:6–26; Alma 42:1–31; Moroni 10:32–33.
  • D&C 45:3–5; 76:40–43, 50–70; Articles of Faith 1:3–4.
  • John Taylor, "The Power of the Atonement for Us Personally," chapter 6.
  • Heber C. Kimball, "Salvation by Works," June 24, 1860, Journal of Discourses 9:75–78.
  • 1 Corinthians 15:12–28; Galatians 2:16–21; Titus 3:3–8.
  • John Taylor, Mediation and Atonement, chapters XVII and XIX.

Questions for research and discussion

  1. Research the responsibilities involved in Ruth 4 using Leviticus 25 and the story itself. What must Boaz be willing and able to do? How does that background illuminate Christ's title without making his work identical to Boaz's?
  2. Trace Paul's comparison between Adam and Christ in Romans 5. Where is there a correspondence, and where does Paul describe an excess of grace? What does that excess contribute to his argument?
  3. If grace abounds where sin increases, why not continue in sin? How does Romans 6 answer its own opening question? What does baptism mean within that answer?
  4. Compare Ephesians 2:8–10, James 2:14–26, and Kimball's sermon. What does each mean by works, and what problem is each addressing? Which apparent disagreements can the contexts explain, and what questions remain?
  5. Alma 42 responds to Corianton's concern about justice. How does Alma explain the relation between justice, mercy, and repentance? Where does Christ enter the argument?
  6. Compare the repeated offerings in Hebrews with Christ's offering. Why does the writer connect the sufficiency of that offering with confidence to approach God? How does D&C 45:3–5 develop that confidence?
  7. Taylor discusses the Atonement's effects on human life. Choose one effect and compare it with Romans 8. What would change in a person's conduct or relationships if that teaching were accepted?
  8. Romans 8 speaks of adoption and joint inheritance with Christ while also speaking of suffering. How can both belong to the same relationship with God? What expectation of discipleship might this correct?
  9. In Moroni 10, coming to Christ includes both relinquishing ungodliness and being perfected by grace. How would you explain the role of personal effort to someone who feels that every failure means Christ has rejected them?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. What makes receiving help or forgiveness difficult? Which account of redemption in these readings helps you understand why someone might resist it, and what could make acceptance possible?
  2. Redemption is something done for a person who cannot manage it alone. When were you last helped, and how did you take it?
