The Good Shepherd

Other Sheep

And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd.

John 10:16

A featured devotional: one talk, given by an invited speaker — to be announced.

About this name

Jesus describes a shepherd who knows his sheep, calls them, and lays down his life for them. His words answer a history of failed shepherding found in Ezekiel 34, where leaders feed themselves while neglecting the flock. The good shepherd's claim is tested through what he does for those entrusted to him.

Jesus also speaks of other sheep who will hear his voice. In 3 Nephi he identifies the people before him as among those other sheep and tells them of still others. His ministry at Bountiful includes teaching, healing, prayer, and the gathering of children. This featured devotional studies the care he gives and the responsibilities his example places on those who help gather and tend his people.

In Ezekiel 34 the LORD says that he will search out the sheep himself once the shepherds of Israel have failed them. This program reads that as Christ speaking in the office he holds, which puts Ezekiel 34 and John 10 in one voice.

Suggested reading

This is a featured devotional, so no cohort is assigned and nothing here is required of anyone. It is for students who want to come to the talk already knowing the texts behind it.

  • Psalm 23; Ezekiel 34:1–24; John 10:1–30.
  • 3 Nephi 11:1–17; 15–18; 21:1–7.
  • 2 Nephi 29:7–14; D&C 18:10–16; 29:1–8.
  • John 21:15–19; Moses 7:28–40.
  • Parley P. Pratt, Key to the Science of Theology (1855), chapter IV, on the scriptural history of the Western Hemisphere.
  • 3 Nephi 20:10–46; 22; Isaiah 49:8–23; D&C 110:11.
  • Orson Pratt, Divine Authenticity of the Book of Mormon, no. 1, numbered paragraphs 1–8, revisiting the argument for additional scripture.

Questions for study

  1. Compare the shepherds in Ezekiel 34 with the shepherd in John 10. What failures does Jesus's description answer? What distinguishes care for a flock from possession of it?
  2. Research the work of shepherding reflected in John 10. What work do the voice, the gate, and the danger to the sheep do in the comparison? Which detail most helps you understand Christ's relationship with his followers?
  3. What do 3 Nephi 15–16 explain about the other sheep, and what do they leave for a future gathering? Compare the account with 2 Nephi 29. How does receiving one scriptural witness affect the responsibility to receive another?
  4. Choose an encounter in 3 Nephi 17 or 18. How does Jesus respond to a need that goes beyond delivering a message? What might a teacher, parent, or friend learn from that response?
  5. Compare Pratt's account of Christ's ministry in the Western Hemisphere with the assigned chapters in 3 Nephi. What does he emphasize in presenting that history? How does it contribute to his larger argument about revelation?
  6. Why does Jesus connect Peter's love for him with feeding his sheep? How could caring for other people reveal something about a person's relationship with Christ that words alone would not?
  7. Jesus speaks of one fold and one shepherd while teaching people in different places. What kind of unity could preserve their particular histories and still make them one people?

Questions for reflection

These are for the speaker, not for the meeting. Write your answers before the discussion or while you are drafting. Nobody will collect them.

  1. What has helped you recognize Christ's care through another person? Which feature of his care could you offer someone who feels overlooked or unwelcome?
  2. He goes after the one. Name someone at this school you have not spoken to this year. Why not?
