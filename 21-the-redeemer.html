Cohort Twenty-One · Week Of April 12 · Eight Talks

The Redeemer

Made Alive in Christ

For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth.

Job 19:25

The talks

This cohort is different. Everywhere else this year we have told what happened. This week we take what the apostles taught about what it did to us. Each of you takes one argument.

  1. Office What a redeemer was in Israelite law. Find out what the goel, the kinsman-redeemer, actually did, who could serve as one, and what he bought back. Leviticus 25:47-55, Ruth 4:1-10, Job 19:25. Then tell us why that is the word for him.
  2. Romans 5 Adam and Christ set side by side as two heads of the human family. By one man came death, by one man came life. Work the comparison Paul is making and be careful with it.
  3. Romans 6 Baptism as a burial. Buried with him by baptism into death, raised to walk in newness of life. Show us that the ordinance is a reenactment and what that means for the person going into the water.
  4. Romans 8 Heirs, and joint-heirs with Christ. Find out what a joint-heir was legally entitled to. Then read verse 17 to the end of the chapter and tell us what it costs.
  5. 1 Corinthians 15 The resurrection chapter, from verse 20 to the end. As in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive. O death, where is thy sting.
  6. Ephesians 2:8-10 By grace are ye saved through faith, not of works. Read verse 10 in the same breath as verse 8, because most people quote one and not the other. Then bring in James 2:14-26 and settle it.
  7. Philippians 2:5-8 He made himself of no reputation, took upon him the form of a servant, and became obedient unto death. Find out what reputation there was to give up, and what the descent cost at each step.
  8. Hebrews 9 and 10 The high priest went behind the veil once a year with blood that was not his own. Work what "once for all" means and why the writer keeps repeating it.

The title

Redeemer is a legal word before it is a religious one. In Israel, if a man fell into debt and lost his land, or sold himself into servitude, the law provided that a near kinsman could step in and buy it back. That man was called the goel, the redeemer. Three things had to be true of him: he had to be related, he had to be able to pay, and he had to be willing. Nobody could be forced into it. That is the whole book of Ruth, when Boaz stands at the gate and takes the role after a nearer kinsman declines it. So when Job says I know that my redeemer liveth, he is not using a vague word of comfort. He is saying there is a kinsman who will buy me back. Hold on to the three conditions all week. He had to be one of us, which is why he was born. He had to be able to pay, which is why he was sinless. And he had to be willing, which is why the first cohort of this year was about a council where he said here am I, send me. Everything the apostles wrote about the atonement is standing on those three.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Romans 5, 6, and 8
  • 1 Corinthians 15
  • Hebrews 9 and 10
  • Philippians 2:5-11 and Ephesians 2:8-10
  • Ruth 4:1-10

Questions for thought

  1. The kinsman-redeemer had to be related, able, and willing. Show each of the three in the Savior's life.
  2. Romans 5 compares Adam and Christ. What exactly is being compared, and where does Paul say the comparison breaks down?
  3. Romans 6 says baptism is a burial. If the ordinance is acting something out, what is a person agreeing to when they go under?
  4. Ephesians 2:8 says not of works, and Ephesians 2:10 says created unto good works. Why do people quote one and not the other?
  5. Read James 2:14-26 beside Ephesians 2:8-10. Are they arguing with each other or answering different questions?
  6. Hebrews says once for all, repeatedly. What was wrong with a sacrifice that had to be repeated every year?
  7. Philippians says he made himself of no reputation. Trace the steps down in verses 6 through 8. How many are there?
  8. Romans 8:17 says joint-heirs with Christ, if so be that we suffer with him. Why is that condition attached?
  9. Paul wrote most of this from prison. Does knowing that change how any of it reads?
