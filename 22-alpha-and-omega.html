Alpha and Omega

This Same Jesus

I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending, saith the Lord, which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty.

Revelation 1:8

The title

We started this year before the world was made, at a council where a Son said here am I, send me. We end it here. Alpha is the first letter of the Greek alphabet and Omega is the last, and the phrase is a way of saying everything from one end to the other, the whole alphabet, and by extension the whole story. He uses it of himself four times in Revelation, and each time he attaches something to it: the beginning and the ending, the first and the last, which is and which was and which is to come. That last one is worth sitting with, because it puts him in all three tenses at once. The subtitle of this cohort comes from the ascension. Two men in white stand next to eleven men staring up at the sky and ask them why they are standing there, and then say this same Jesus shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go. Not a different one. The same one. The last week of this school year covers everyone who has seen him since, and the promise that the list is not closed.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • Acts 1:1-11
  • Acts 7:51-60 and 9:1-22
  • Revelation 1:1-20
  • Joseph Smith History 1:14-20 and D&C 110
  • D&C 45:44-59

Questions for thought

  1. The angels ask the disciples why they stand gazing up. What is the rebuke in that question?
  2. This same Jesus. Why would the angels emphasize that word, and what would be lost without it?
  3. Stephen says he sees the Son of man standing. Everywhere else in scripture he is described as seated. Find out what people have made of that difference.
  4. Saul held the coats at Stephen's death and then met the Lord himself on the road. What do you think he remembered afterward?
  5. Paul says last of all he was seen of me also. He never met the mortal Jesus. Does that make his witness weaker or stronger?
  6. Compare the accounts in Acts 9, Acts 7, and Joseph Smith History 1. What do they have in common, and where do they differ?
  7. Matthew 24:34 says this generation shall not pass. Find out how that verse has been read and pick one you can defend.
  8. 2 Peter says one day is with the Lord as a thousand years. Is that an answer to the delay or a way around the question?
  9. This is the last talk of the school year. What do you want this student body to be left holding?
