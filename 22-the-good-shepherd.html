Cohort Twenty-Two · Week Of April 19 · Six Talks

The Good Shepherd

Other Sheep

And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd.

John 10:16

The talks

  1. Office John 10:1-18. Find out what a shepherd in that country actually did, what a sheepfold was, how a shepherd's own sheep knew his voice out of a mixed flock, and what a hireling was. Then work verse 16, which is where this cohort gets its name.
  2. Restoration 3 Nephi 15:11-24 and 16:1-3. He tells the Nephites plainly that they are the other sheep he meant, and then says there are still others they have not been told about. Show us what that does to John 10:16.
  3. Text 3 Nephi 11:1-17. A small voice they could not at first understand, heard three times. Then the descent, and a multitude of twenty-five hundred going forth one by one to feel the prints in his hands and feet.
  4. World Bountiful, and the condition of the people standing there. Go back to 3 Nephi 8 through 10 and find out what they had just survived, how long the darkness lasted, who had died, and what the survivors had been doing in the months before he came.
  5. Witness Nephi, in 3 Nephi 11:18-22, called out of the multitude by name and given authority to baptize. Take him through the questions on the facing page.
  6. Objection Why would he go anywhere else, and why is none of this in the Bible? Take the objection seriously and answer it from John 10:16, Ezekiel 37:15-20, and 2 Nephi 29:7-14.

The title

He gives this teaching in Jerusalem, in the middle of an argument, and it is worth knowing how shepherding actually worked before you read it. Several flocks would be kept in one fold overnight with a keeper at the door. In the morning each shepherd came and called, and his own sheep separated themselves out of the mixed flock and followed him, because they knew the voice. That is the picture behind verse 16. And then he says something that must have sounded strange to everyone standing there: other sheep I have, which are not of this fold. He does not say he might have. He says he has them, and that he must bring them, and that they shall hear his voice. Centuries later, on the other side of the world, he stood in front of survivors at Bountiful and told them they were the ones he meant, and then told them there were still others. This is the second-to-last cohort of the year, and it is here to make one point: the shepherd went and got them. He did not wait at the fold.

Required reading, for every speaker in this cohort

  • John 10:1-30
  • 3 Nephi 11:1-41
  • 3 Nephi 15:11-24 and 16:1-3
  • 3 Nephi 8:5-23 and 10:1-10
  • Ezekiel 37:15-20

Questions for thought

  1. Find out how a shepherd separated his own sheep out of a mixed fold. What does that say about how he expects to be followed?
  2. He says the hireling flees because he is a hireling and cares not for the sheep. What is the difference between a hireling and a shepherd, and how would you tell them apart?
  3. In 3 Nephi 11 the voice is heard three times before anyone understands it. Why did it take three?
  4. Twenty-five hundred people went forth one by one. Find out how long that would take. Why do it that way instead of all at once?
  5. Read 3 Nephi 8 through 10 first. What condition were those people in when he arrived, and how does that change the way you read chapter 11?
  6. He tells the Nephites there are still other sheep besides them. What do you do with a promise that has not been fully explained yet?
  7. 2 Nephi 29 answers people who say a Bible is enough. Summarize the argument in your own words.
  8. He said they shall hear my voice. What does it mean to hear a voice you have never heard before and know whose it is?
  9. Where do you actually recognize his voice, and how do you tell it from your own thinking?
