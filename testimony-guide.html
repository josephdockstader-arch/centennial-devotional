Building and Bearing a Testimony

A study guide for the closing of every talk

This is the long version of the helps on the Bearing Your Testimony page. It gathers what the scriptures, Joseph Smith, the early Apostles, and the Priesthood Discourses teach about what a testimony is, how a person gets one, why it has to be spoken, how to speak it, and how it is kept or lost. Every quotation comes from one of the source collections described at the end of the page. Read it straight through, or go to the section you need.

1. What a testimony is

A testimony is a witness given by the Holy Ghost to your own spirit. Every source collection gives the same definition. It is revelation, given to one person, and the person who has it knows something.

How shall we know that we obey Him? There is but one method by which we can know it, and that is by the inspiration of the Spirit of the Lord witnessing unto our spirit that we are His, that we love Him, and that He loves us. It is by the spirit of revelation we know this. We have no witness to ourselves internally, without the spirit of revelation.

Brigham Young, Journal of Discourses 12:114 (November 17, 1867)

Alma described how he came to know for himself. When he asks his listeners how he knows what he is preaching, he does not point to an argument or to what other people told him.

Do ye not suppose that I know of these things myself? Behold, I testify unto you that I do know that these things whereof I have spoken, are true. And how do ye suppose that I know of their surety? Behold, I say unto you, they are made known unto me by the holy spirit of God. Behold, I have fasted and prayed many days that I might know these things of myself. And now I do know of myself that they are true; for the Lord God hath made them manifest unto me by his Holy Spirit; and this is the spirit of revelation which is in me.

Alma 5:45–46

Joseph Smith built his definition on one line from John the Revelator, "the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" (Revelation 19:10), and followed it to its conclusion. Anyone who holds a real testimony holds the spirit of revelation, and a person who does not receive revelation for himself is in danger.

John the Revelator says that the testimony of Jesus is the Spirit of prophecy. Now if any man has the testimony of Jesus, has he not the spirit of prophecy? and if he has the spirit of prophecy, I ask, "Is he not a prophet? and if a prophet, will he, can he receive revelation?" And any man that does not receive revelation for himself must be damned for the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy, for Christ says, "Ask and you shall receive."

Joseph Smith, Nauvoo, July 9, 1843 (James Burgess notebook), Forgotten Teachings of Joseph Smith, p. 37

He also tied testimony to salvation itself: "Salvation cannot come without revelation... No man can be a minister of Jesus Christ except he has the testimony of Jesus; and this is the spirit of prophecy. Whenever salvation has been administered, it has been by testimony" (Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith, p. 160).

A testimony that belongs to someone else, or to a past generation, does not save anyone in this one. Joseph Smith put it in one sentence: "No Generation was ever saved or destroyed upon dead testimony, neither can be, but by Living" (Forgotten Teachings, p. 480). Brigham Young pushed the same point as far as it goes. He said he "would not give you the ashes of a rye straw for every word that is contained in the Bible, Book of Mormon and Doctrine and Covenants, so far as their efficacy is concerned to save any man, independent of the living Priesthood of the Son of God, and the testimony of the Holy Ghost in the heart of the believer" (Journal of Discourses 11:331). The scriptures point a person to the witness, and it is the witness that saves.

The Priesthood Discourses quote Brigham Young's standard and hold everyone to it: "Let every one get a knowledge for himself that this work is true. We do not want you to say that it is true until you know that it is; and if you know it, that knowledge is as good to you as though the Lord came down and told you" (quoted in Priesthood Discourses, 1971, p. 144). President Alma A. Timpson described what such a witness does for the person who has it: "when the revelation of the Spirit of God bears witness to your spirit through the power and influence of the Holy Ghost you do know, and all the adversity in Earth and hell combined can't make it otherwise" (1971, p. 77). And President John W. Woolley's line, preserved by J. Marion Hammon: "The gospel is true, whether I believe it or not" (1968, p. 254).

2. How the witness comes

The sources are specific about how the Spirit speaks. Knowing how the Spirit speaks helps, because a person who expects something dramatic can miss a quiet answer.

  • In the mind and in the heart. "I will tell you in your mind and in your heart by the Holy Ghost which shall come upon you and which shall dwell in your heart. Now, behold, this is the spirit of revelation" (Doctrine and Covenants 8:2–3).
  • A burning in the bosom. "You must study it out in your mind; then, you must ask me if it be right, and if it is right, I will cause that your bosom shall burn within you; therefore, you shall feel that it is right" (Doctrine and Covenants 9:8). Brigham Young described his own: "it seemed as though a live coal was in my heart, that caused it to burn with joy and gladness" (Journal of Discourses 13:149).
  • Peace spoken to the mind. "Did I not speak peace to your mind concerning the matter? What greater witness can you have than from God?" (Doctrine and Covenants 6:22–23).
  • Light, and a soul that enlarges. "It beginneth to enlarge my soul; yea, it beginneth to enlighten my understanding; yea, and it beginneth to be delicious to me" (Alma 32:28). Joseph Smith taught that the Holy Ghost "has no other effect than pure intelligence... expanding the mind, enlightening the understanding, and storing the intellect with present knowledge" (Teachings, pp. 149–151).
  • A still voice of perfect mildness. "It was not a voice of thunder, neither was it a voice of a great tumultuous noise; but behold, it was a still voice of perfect mildness, as if it had been a whisper, and it did pierce even to the very soul" (Helaman 5:30).
  • Sudden strokes of ideas. "When you feel pure intelligence flowing into you, it may give you sudden strokes of ideas... and thus by learning the Spirit of God and understanding it, you may grow into the principle of revelation" (Teachings, p. 151).

Two accounts show the range. Enos, after a day and a night of prayer: "the voice of the Lord came into my mind again" (Enos 1:10). Ebenezer Robinson, receiving a witness of the Book of Mormon, said "a ball of fire came down from above and striking the top of my head passed down into my heart" (Forgotten Teachings). Elder Matthias Cowley compared the gifts of the Spirit to a current of electricity. The people who receive them "feel and know, beyond the shadow of a doubt, that some power is there over which they have no absolute control and which is not a part of their own being" (Improvement Era 2:447–451).

Most testimonies begin far more quietly than any of these. One anthology preserves this counsel: "The faint dawn of the Spirit is even now upon your mind. Now, reader, cherish this little dawn of light until the daylight of more truth shines more clearly upon your mind" (Ogden Kraut, Sermons and Anthologies, pp. 54–55; also Truth Magazine 1:310).

The witness is also open to anyone who seeks it. President Guy H. Musser, pointing to Cornelius in Acts 10: "The Holy Ghost is not limited to members of the Church. The Holy Ghost is not limited to members bearing the Holy Priesthood. The Holy Ghost is to lead us into all truth and is given to the sons and daughters of God on condition of repentance... a witness and a testator to the truthfulness of the Gospel" (Priesthood Discourses, 1968, p. 378).

3. What a testimony is a witness of

A testimony is a witness of something. The sources name the same things again and again, and they are the same things a person declares when bearing it.

  • God the Father and Jesus Christ. That God lives, and that Jesus is the Christ, the resurrected Redeemer. "The Holy Ghost beareth record of the Father and me" (3 Nephi 11:32). Joseph Smith, with his hand raised: "I am a witness that there is a God, for I saw Him in open day while praying in a silent grove in the spring of 1820" (Forgotten Teachings, Edward Stevenson record). Joseph Smith and Sidney Rigdon, after the vision of the three glories: "this is the testimony, last of all, which we give of him, that he lives; for we saw him, even on the right hand of God" (Doctrine and Covenants 76:22–23).
  • Joseph Smith's calling. That he was called of God as prophet, seer, and revelator of the dispensation of the fullness of times, and that he stands as "the Witness and Testator of this dispensation" (Truth Magazine 1:186; 4:27; Doctrine and Covenants 5:2; 135:3).
  • The Book of Mormon and the revelations. That the record was translated "by the gift and power of God," and that the revelations given through Joseph Smith are true. The Lord commands the Saints to bear record of them (Doctrine and Covenants 17:1–6; 20:10–13; 67:4–9; the Testimony of the Three Witnesses).
  • The Priesthood and its keys. That heavenly messengers restored the authority of God, and that in its ordinances "the power of godliness is manifest" (Doctrine and Covenants 84:19–22; Truth Magazine 5:248).
  • The work and the Kingdom. That God has set his hand to gather Israel, build Zion, and administer the ordinances for the living and the dead (Journal of Discourses, many sermons; Ogden Kraut, Kingdom and Government, ch. 14).
  • The fullness of the gospel. Truth Magazine describes a complete last-days testimony as one that takes in all the restored celestial laws, and names the United Order and the New and Everlasting Covenant of Celestial Marriage (Truth Magazine 5:1; 10:25; 15:205; 20:213). The Book of Mormon and the Journal of Discourses passages on testimony list the items above without this one.

The Priesthood Discourses of 1960 to 1975 name four pillars: the Father and the Son; Joseph Smith as prophet and as Witness and Testator of the Father and the Son; the Holy Priesthood and its unbroken line of keyholders from Joseph Smith, Brigham Young, and John Taylor down to the presiding council; and the fullness of celestial law, including the New and Everlasting Covenant, the United Order, and the 1886 revelation to John Taylor (1962, p. 268; 1964, pp. 27–28; 1975, p. 143). They also draw a line between a testimony of a principle and a testimony of the Priesthood keys, and warn that the first without the second leaves a person "deluded and without saving keys" (1964, p. 28; 1968, p. 357).

4. Belief, faith, testimony, knowledge

The sources treat these four words as a progression, one rung at a time. Knowing which rung you are on tells you what to do next.

  • Belief is giving credit to the testimony of others, and being willing to "give place" for the word (Lectures on Faith, Lecture 2, paragraph 56; Alma 32:27). Belief by itself does not save. Heber C. Kimball: "the gospel is the power of God unto all that obey, not unto all that believe, for the devils believe" (Journal of Discourses 4:82).
  • Faith is belief put into action, "the moving cause of all action" and "the principle of power" (Lectures on Faith, Lecture 1). It is hope "for things which are not seen, which are true" (Alma 32:21). Heber C. Kimball again: "What is faith? It is confidence. What is confidence? It is faith. Some people are striving and striving to get faith, when saving faith is simply confidence in God, flowing from walking in obedience to His commandments" (Journal of Discourses 4:248).
  • Testimony is the witness of the Holy Ghost that confirms what was believed and hoped, and it comes after the effort, never before it. "Ye receive no witness until after the trial of your faith" (Ether 12:6). Erastus Snow describes the moment: the Holy Ghost confirms what a person already believed, "giving us a positive assurance of their correctness, and through it we obtain a personal knowledge, not as one that has been told, but as one that has seen, felt, heard, and that knows for himself" (Journal of Discourses 21:308–310).
  • Knowledge is what remains when the witness is complete in a thing. "Your knowledge is perfect in that thing, and your faith is dormant" (Alma 32:34). Oliver Cowdery, of the angel he saw: "I did see this; I know I saw it, and faith has nothing to do with it, as a perfect knowledge has swallowed up the faith which I had in the work" (Truth Magazine 9:109–110). Joseph Smith: "Knowledge does away with darkness, suspense and doubt; for these cannot exist where knowledge is" (Teachings, p. 288).

A caution about that last rung. Alma limits it carefully: the knowledge is perfect "in that thing," meaning the one thing the witness confirmed, and two verses later he says the work is not finished. "Now behold, after ye have tasted this light is your knowledge perfect? Behold I say unto you, Nay; neither must ye lay aside your faith, for ye have only exercised your faith to plant the seed" (Alma 32:35–36). The tree still has to be nourished, or it withers (32:37–38). So a testimony does not replace faith. Spiritual experience strengthens knowledge in a particular thing, while continued faith and nourishment remain necessary for everything else.

Joseph Smith warned that reading about someone else's experience will not move you up the ladder: "reading the experience of others, or the revelation given to them, can never give us a comprehensive view of our condition and true relation to God" (Teachings, p. 324). The Priesthood Discourses give the ladder in Brigham Young's order, "The beginning of faith is belief. Acting on belief, we obey. On obeying, we then have the gift and power, which is called faith" (1966, p. 8), and Joseph Zitting draws the practical conclusion: "Testimonies are made by practice, not by reading a book, believing it, and then going about doing something else. We build our testimonies by practicing the things we believe" (1968, p. 37).

5. No borrowed light

The sources give three reasons a personal testimony is required, and the first one is repeated in every collection.

A test is coming that will require a testimony of your own. Heber C. Kimball said it to the Saints near the end of his life.

To meet the difficulties that are coming, it will be necessary for you to have a knowledge of the truth of this work for yourselves. The difficulties will be of such a character that the man or woman who does not possess this personal knowledge or witness will fall. If you have not got the testimony, live right and call upon the Lord and cease not till you obtain it. If you do not you will not stand... The time will come when no man nor woman will be able to endure on borrowed light. Each will have to be guided by the light within himself.

Heber C. Kimball, Life of Heber C. Kimball, pp. 460–461

Shortly before his death he added, "Oh, the test, the test, the test, who will be able to stand?" When someone asked what the test was, he answered, "I don't know, but it is only those that know that Jesus is the Christ that can stand" (Truth Magazine 8:267). Joseph F. Smith said it in a single line in the Salt Lake Temple: "Every tub will have to stand on its own bottom" (Truth Magazine 3:63). The Priesthood Discourses preserve the same figure from President John W. Woolley: "Brother, get a testimony, get a testimony. Time will come when every man will stand on his own testimony, like a tub will stand on its own bottom, or he won't stand" (1968, p. 254).

Those discourses come back to the point year after year. "There are many ladies who are hoping that their husbands know what they're talking about, and they are living in borrowed light in regard to these things. There are many Elders who are living on borrowed light" (1965, p. 308). "We cannot rely on the testimonies of our fathers and mothers and say we know that Jesus is the Christ. We have to have a burning testimony within us" (1972, p. 417). "If I'm content with leaning upon someone else, in the final analysis, there won't be much hope for me, no matter what a posterity I may leave" (1960, p. 2). And the consequence: "if there are those among us who have not and don't obtain that divine testimony borne of the Spirit of God by the spirit of revelation, the time will come when we'll be severely shaken and may not be able to stand" (1963, p. 118).

Exaltation depends on being valiant in it. The vision of the three glories divides the celestial from the terrestrial on this point. The terrestrial are "they who received the testimony of Jesus, and believed on his name... these are they who are not valiant in the testimony of Jesus. Wherefore, they obtained not the crown over the kingdom of our God" (Doctrine and Covenants 76:51, 79). Receiving the testimony is only the first requirement. The crown is given to those who are valiant in it.

A personal witness holds up under challenge. Jacob, facing Sherem: "I truly had seen angels, and they had ministered unto me. And also, I had heard the voice of the Lord speaking unto me in very word... wherefore, I could not be shaken" (Jacob 7:5). Orson Pratt taught that the natural senses can be fooled by "jugglery, optics, or natural illusions," but that the witness of the Holy Ghost cannot deceive (Journal of Discourses 14:244–246). Those who have taken the Holy Spirit for their guide "shall not be hewn down and cast into the fire, but shall abide the day" (Doctrine and Covenants 45:57).

6. Building a testimony

Every collection lays out the same sequence, and every one of them says it is work. The Lord's instruction to Oliver Cowdery describes the whole process in one sentence: "you must study it out in your mind; then, you must ask me if it be right" (Doctrine and Covenants 9:8).

  • Desire, even a particle. "If ye will awake and arouse your faculties even to an experiment upon my words, and exercise a particle of faith; yea, even if ye can no more than desire to believe, let this desire work in you" (Alma 32:27). The Priesthood Discourses: "Desire, to me, is far, far more than a mere wish or wishful thinking. Desire is translated into action. If we desire things in righteousness, God will grant them through our faith" (1968, p. 61).
  • Repent, and make things right with people. Bishop John H. Koyle sat in a meeting at Leland, Utah, and heard the speaker say that if a person prays and gets no witness, "it is because you have not repented enough to make things right between you, the Lord and your brethren." He went home, made amends with his neighbors, and the witness came that same night (Ogden Kraut, Relief Mine, pp. 26–27).
  • Search the scriptures and think about them. "They had searched the scriptures diligently, that they might know the word of God" (Alma 17:2). "Remember how merciful the Lord hath been... and ponder it in your hearts" (Moroni 10:3). To Hyrum Smith: "seek not to declare my word but first seek to obtain my word" (Doctrine and Covenants 11:21). The Priesthood Discourses describe the kind of reading meant: "not only reading, but studying, studying what we read and thrashing over and over, again and again, these little verses... shutting our eyes and thinking about it... Wrestling with God!" (1963, p. 63).
  • Pray plainly, in secret, for what you want, and keep at it. Joseph Smith: "Be plain and simple and ask for what you want, just like you would go to a neighbor and say, I want to borrow your horse to go to the mill" (Forgotten Teachings, p. 27). Moroni's promise: "if ye shall ask with a sincere heart, with real intent, having faith in Christ, he will manifest the truth of it unto you by the power of the Holy Ghost" (Moroni 10:4). Enos prayed "all the day long" and into the night (Enos 1:4). The Priesthood Discourses: "Faith, brothers and sisters, is the exertion of the mind. I can't think of a more effective way to exert and apply this power... than through the ability to pray... learn how to pray and then bring our prayers to fruition through obedience" (1969, p. 374).
  • Fast. Joseph Smith taught on July 30, 1840, that "every family should get power by fasting and prayer" (Forgotten Teachings, John Smith record). His father, Joseph Smith Sr., fasted from sunrise until four in the afternoon in the Kirtland Temple to draw down the Spirit (Truth Magazine 1:86).
  • Obey. Do the will, and then you know. "If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine" (John 7:17) is quoted in three of the collections. John Taylor taught that spiritual law works like natural law: "sow wheat and you reap wheat; plant corn and you gather corn" (Journal of Discourses 23:48). Heber C. Kimball compared it to digging a well: the labor brings the water, and the water brings the knowledge (Journal of Discourses 4:247). "If ye will not harden your hearts, and ask me in faith, believing that ye shall receive, with diligence in keeping my commandments, surely these things shall be made known unto you" (1 Nephi 15:11). Carl O. N. Holm: "Faith grows and increases through obedience" (Priesthood Discourses, 1965, p. 338). And the invitation: "If there's any question in our minds about this, if our testimonies are incomplete... Let's put it to the test and ask the Lord about it. If we are eager and sincere, we'll get our answer" (1961, p. 38).
  • Experiment on the word, and hold on through the trial. Plant the seed, nourish it, and watch it swell (Alma 32:28–43). "Ye receive no witness until after the trial of your faith" (Ether 12:6). "After much tribulation, cometh the blessings" (Doctrine and Covenants 58:4). George Q. Cannon: "Every law of the Gospel has a trial connected with it, and the higher the law the greater the trial" (Journal of Discourses 24:221).
  • Sacrifice. "A religion that does not require the sacrifice of all things never has power sufficient to produce the faith necessary unto life and salvation... it is through the medium of the sacrifice of all earthly things that men do actually know that they are doing the things that are well pleasing in the sight of God" (Lectures on Faith, Lecture 6, paragraph 7).

Ordinances open the channel. Ogden Kraut's compilation keeps a distinction worth knowing. The convincing power of the Holy Ghost visits anyone who sincerely seeks, which is how Cornelius received it in Acts 10 and how Moroni 10:4 works for a reader of any faith. The Gift of the Holy Ghost is given by the laying on of hands after baptism, and it carries a standing right to the Spirit's company (Kingdom and Government, ch. 14, pp. 147–148, 158). Nephi describes the order: "witnessing unto the Father that ye are willing to take upon you the name of Christ, by baptism... then shall ye receive the Holy Ghost; yea, then cometh the baptism of fire and of the Holy Ghost" (2 Nephi 31:13). When Joseph Smith instructed Mercy Rachel Thompson before her endowment he told her, "This will bring you out of darkness into marvelous light" (Forgotten Teachings).

It grows line upon line. No source expects a testimony to arrive all at once. Joseph Smith's counsel to John Taylor describes how it grows.

Elder Taylor, you have been baptized, you have had hands laid upon your head for the reception of the Holy Ghost, and you have been ordained to the holy Priesthood. Now, if you will continue to follow the leadings of that spirit, it will always lead you right. Sometimes it might be contrary to your judgment; never mind that, follow its dictates; and if you be true to its whisperings, it will build you up... until by and by it will become in you a principle of revelation, so that you will know all things as they transpire.

Joseph Smith to John Taylor, Forgotten Teachings of Joseph Smith, p. 676

"He that receiveth light and continueth in God receiveth more light, and that light groweth brighter and brighter until the perfect day" (Doctrine and Covenants 50:24). Brigham Young said it becomes a "well of water, springing up unto everlasting life" inside a person (Journal of Discourses 13:149).

How you know you have received it. The sources name the fruit.

  • Peace of conscience, with guilt swept away. Enos: his guilt was swept away because "God could not lie" (Enos 1:1–8). King Benjamin's people (Mosiah 4:1–3).
  • The word swells, sprouts, and becomes delicious, and your understanding is enlightened (Alma 32:28, 34).
  • A burning in the bosom, or peace spoken to the mind (Doctrine and Covenants 9:8; 6:22–23).
  • A mighty change of heart, with "no more disposition to do evil, but to do good continually" (Mosiah 5:2).
  • Doubt and fear leave. Oliver Cowdery, after the visit of John the Baptist: "Where was room for doubt? Nowhere; uncertainty had fled, doubt had sunk no more to rise" (Ogden Kraut, Sermons and Anthologies, pp. 8–9).
  • Joy. Alma: "my soul was filled with joy as exceeding as was my pain" (Alma 36:20).

People who obtained it. The accounts below come from the sources. The same things repeat in them: a real question, a decision to find out, private prayer, effort that continued past the first day, and a witness that came after the exertion rather than before it.

  • Joseph Smith. Troubled by the contention among the churches, he read James 1:5, went into the grove with "a fixed determination to obtain," prayed out loud, was seized by a darkness, and then saw the Father and the Son (Forgotten Teachings; Journal of Discourses 21:313–315).
  • Enos. Prayed "all the day long" and into the night for his own soul. The voice came into his mind, and his guilt was swept away (Enos 1:1–8).
  • Alma the Younger. Three days of torment, then he remembered his father's words about Christ and cried for mercy. Afterward he "fasted and prayed many days" to know for himself (Mosiah 27; Alma 36; Alma 5:46).
  • King Lamoni. Heard Ammon teach from the scriptures, and "the dark veil of unbelief was being cast away from his mind... the light of everlasting life was lit up in his soul" (Alma 19:6).
  • Oliver Cowdery. Cried to God in his heart one night, alone, before he had ever met Joseph Smith. The Lord later reminded him: "Did I not speak peace to your mind?" (Doctrine and Covenants 6:22–23).
  • Brigham Young. Examined the Book of Mormon "studiously for two full years" before deciding, praying that his feet would not be entangled in error. When light reflected on his understanding, he "drank it in" (Journal of Discourses 14:135–136).
  • Lucy Walker Kimball. Her feelings revolted at a principle Joseph Smith taught, and he promised her a witness if she would pray sincerely. At dawn, between three haystacks, hands raised, an angel came, and "calm, sweet peace" settled her for the rest of her life (Truth Magazine 14:300; 20:350).
  • Orson F. Whitney. Went on a mission with "just enough faith to be willing to go and fill it." Praying in the field, he received a witness he valued "as more precious than life" (Truth Magazine 5:71).
  • J. Marion Hammon. As a young man he resolved to read the Book of Mormon and kept stopping at page 64. When he renewed his determination and pushed through, "he kindled an internal fire and gained an unshakeable testimony" (Priesthood Discourses, 1967, p. 64).

7. Why a testimony has to be spoken

A testimony is not given for private comfort only. The sources give five reasons it has to be spoken, and the last of them is that the speaking is where it grows.

You covenanted to. The baptismal covenant at the Waters of Mormon includes the promise "to stand as witnesses of God at all times and in all things, and in all places that ye may be in, even until death" (Mosiah 18:9). Standing as a witness is the wording of the covenant everyone made in the water.

The Lord is displeased when it is hidden.

But with some I am not well pleased, for they will not open their mouths, but hide the talent which I have given unto them because of the fear of man. Wo unto such, for mine anger is kindled against them. And it shall come to pass, if they are not more faithful unto me, it shall be taken away, even that which they have.

Doctrine and Covenants 60:2–3

The warning is that a testimony hidden out of fear can be taken away. Mary Elizabeth Rollins Lightner, who received spiritual gifts in Nauvoo, stopped using them after the Saints were driven out, and wrote simply, "I lost the gift after we were driven" (Forgotten Teachings). Truth Magazine names the mechanism: keeping a testimony quiet out of fear of "popular disfavor or social ostracism" drives the Holy Ghost away and lets spiritual "dry rot" set in (14:275).

It is recorded in heaven, and it brings forgiveness. "Your testimony... is recorded in heaven for the angels to look upon; and they rejoice over you, and your sins are forgiven you" (Doctrine and Covenants 62:3). The Lord ties forgiveness directly to the practice: "I will forgive you of your sins with this commandment, that you remain steadfast in your minds in solemnity and the spirit of prayer, in bearing testimony to all the world of those things which are communicated unto you" (84:61).

Every warned person must warn. "I sent you out to testify and warn the people, and it becometh every man who hath been warned to warn his neighbor. Therefore, they are left without excuse, and their sins are upon their own heads" (Doctrine and Covenants 88:81). Both the Journal of Discourses and Ogden Kraut describe a testimony as a legal witness. Accepted, it brings salvation to the hearer. Rejected, it leaves the hearer accountable and clears the bearer's garments of their blood.

A testimony grows in the bearing of it. The Journal of Discourses preserves the case of an elder who had never publicly said he knew Joseph Smith was a prophet. He found himself cornered before a crowded meeting.

Feeling he must meet the issue, he opened his mouth and uttered the words: "Joseph is a Prophet." Instantly his tongue was loosened, the Holy Ghost poured out upon him, and from that hour he possessed a powerful, permanent witness.

Journal of Discourses 11:87

Almera Woodward had the same experience opening her mouth "with fear and trembling" to teach her sister: "the light of the Lord shone upon my understanding and my tongue was loosed," and both sisters were converted at once (Truth Magazine 5:150; 16:235). Ogden Kraut states the rule: sharing a testimony "causes it to burn brighter, expanding the soul and inviting greater gifts of the Spirit" (Sermons and Anthologies, p. 33). When a testimony is borne by the Spirit, "the power of the Holy Ghost carrieth it unto the hearts of the children of men" (2 Nephi 33:1).

The Priesthood Discourses add a warning about the reverse: "If he gets a testimony, by the gift and power of God, then to do anything contrary to that is to insult the giver of it... Instead of its growing in your heart, it'll diminish, and, one day, you'll wake up" (1961, p. 179). They also recommend putting a testimony into writing: "This matter of being able to present our testimonies, to write them down, to get them in a phraseology that's good for us... is a fine thing... in the preparation of this, there comes a mental training... to commit it to paper, to look at it, to study it, ponder over it... is going to help us to live that testimony even better" (1963, p. 136).

Truth Magazine's summary: "one living, burning honest testimony of a righteous God-fearing man that Jesus is the Christ and that Joseph was God's prophet, is worth a thousand books and lectures aimed at debasing the Gospel" (4:117).

8. How to bear it

The sources are practical here. The instructions come from Joseph Smith, Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball, and the revelations, and they agree with one another.

  • By the Spirit, or not at all. "If ye receive not the Spirit ye shall not teach" (Doctrine and Covenants 42:14). Joseph Smith: "no man can preach the Gospel without the Holy Ghost" (Teachings, p. 112). Speak the thoughts put into your heart "in the very moment" (100:5–6), and the Holy Ghost will bear record to the people listening (100:8).
  • Plainly. "My soul delighteth in plainness" (2 Nephi 31:3; 25:4). "In solemnity of heart, in the spirit of meekness" (Doctrine and Covenants 100:7), and "in mildness" (38:41). Joseph Smith warned the Twelve to "beware of Gentile sophistry" (Teachings, p. 156).
  • In your own natural voice. Joseph Smith told his brother Don Carlos to "always speak in his natural tone of voice and not keep in a loud or unnatural strain," and he made fun of the sanctimonious "long ass-like tone" of the sectarian preachers (Forgotten Teachings, p. 150; Heber C. Kimball, Journal of Discourses 3:66–67).
  • Briefly. Brigham Young and Heber C. Kimball taught that five words spoken by the power of the Holy Ghost do more good than hours of uninspired talking (Journal of Discourses 11:87). Joseph Smith: "It is a day of warning, and not of many words" (Teachings, p. 156). J. Marion Hammon's father: "Learn to stand up, speak up, and shut up... for there's only one thing that can edify the saints of God: the Spirit of God" (Priesthood Discourses, 1961, p. 118).
  • Clean. Daniel H. Wells: "No testimony against evil can be given with power by any man who is under the influence of that evil... we must be clean every whit. Then from the depths of a pure soul will come a testimony that will burn where it goes" (Millennial Star 27:73–75; Truth Magazine 17:90).
  • Humbly, with the glory to God. Joseph Smith taught that when the Spirit works through a person, "let that man or those men take care that they do not ascribe the glory unto themselves, but be careful that they are humble, and ascribe the praise and glory to God and the Lamb" (Teachings, pp. 155–156; Priesthood Discourses, 1965, p. 443).
  • Boldly. Joseph Smith raised his hand toward heaven when he bore solemn witness of the First Vision. Nathan Tanner, who heard him: "O how these words thrilled my entire system, and filled me with joy unspeakable to behold one who... could with boldness testify that he had been in the presence of Jesus Christ!" (Forgotten Teachings).

What to include. The content of a testimony is the list in section 3, stated as plain first-person declarations: that God lives; that Jesus is the Christ, and that his atonement and resurrection are real; that Joseph Smith was called of God; that the Book of Mormon is true; that the Priesthood was restored; and that repentance and baptism are required (Doctrine and Covenants 11:9; 58:47; Ogden Kraut, Sermons and Anthologies, pp. 227–229). Nephi's description of what the Saints talk about: "we talk of Christ, we rejoice in Christ, we preach of Christ, we prophesy of Christ" (2 Nephi 25:26).

A simple shape, drawn from the sources.

  • Say what you know, one or two of the pillars, in the first person, as Alma did: "I do know of myself that they are true."
  • If the Spirit prompts, say briefly how you came to know it: the prayer, the study, the trial, the moment of peace. Alma named his: "I have fasted and prayed many days." Not every testimony needs this part.
  • Say what it has done in you, such as more peace, a change of heart, or a desire to do good. That is the evidence Mosiah 5:2 and Alma 32 describe.
  • Close in the name of Jesus Christ. The Three Witnesses closed their testimony this way: "the honor be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, which is one God. Amen."

What to leave out, as the sources name it.

  • Contention and argument. "He that hath the spirit of contention is not of me, but is of the devil" (3 Nephi 11:29; Doctrine and Covenants 60:14).
  • Long doctrinal lectures and "mysteries." A testimony is a declaration, and a sermon is a different thing (Journal of Discourses; Ogden Kraut, Priesthood and Authority, p. 206).
  • Speculation past what you know by revelation (Teachings, p. 156).
  • Boasting, self-justification, gossip, and flattery (Doctrine and Covenants 60:14; 2 Nephi 28:12–15).
  • Priestcraft, which is preaching for gain or praise (2 Nephi 26:29).
  • Public confession of private sins. Brigham Young: "If you have sinned against your God, or against yourselves, confess to God, and keep the matter to yourselves, for I do not want to know anything about it" (Journal of Discourses 8:83–88).
  • Forced emotion or a pious voice. "Tears have nothing to do with the truth... Sincerity is no proof of anything" (Priesthood Discourses, 1961, p. 154).
  • Saying you know what you only hope, or agreeing out loud with something you doubt inside. The Priesthood Discourses call this false witnessing: "She is falsifying the feelings of her heart... the Spirit of God will not possess the mind and will of a person who is a false witness" (1965, pp. 325–328). Say exactly what is true for you.
  • Sacred personal experiences and family matters in front of a hostile or careless audience (Priesthood Discourses, 1960, p. 198; 1966, p. 38; 1974, p. 253).

The duty reaches past the meeting. Joseph Smith's charge to the Twelve applies to every witness: "Ye are not sent out to be taught, but to teach. Let every word be seasoned with grace. Be vigilant; be sober. It is a day of warning, and not of many words. Act honestly before God and man... Be honest, open, and frank in all your intercourse with mankind" (Teachings, p. 156). The collections keep the record of witnesses who held their testimony in courtrooms and prisons. Oliver Cowdery, taunted in a Michigan courtroom about Joseph Smith and "the golden Bible," stood and answered: "I saw the angel and heard his voice, how can I deny it? It happened in the daytime when the sun was shining bright in the firmament... Now how can I deny it, I dare not; I will not!" (Ogden Kraut, Personal Letters, Pamphlets, Misc., pp. 3–5).

9. Keeping it, and how testimonies are lost

A testimony can be lost. The sources name the causes precisely, and every one of them can be avoided. They also name the daily practices that keep a testimony, and they are the same practices that built it.

How testimonies are lost.

  • Sin, and sexual sin in particular. "He that looketh on a woman to lust after her, or if any shall commit adultery in their hearts, they shall not have the spirit, but shall deny the faith and shall fear" (Doctrine and Covenants 63:16). George A. Smith recalled one of Joseph Smith's revelations, that a man who commits adultery "shall lose the Spirit of the Lord and deny the faith" (Forgotten Teachings, pp. 558, 561).
  • Treating lightly what you have received. "Your minds in times past have been darkened because of unbelief and because you have treated lightly the things you have received, which vanity and unbelief hath brought the whole church under condemnation" (Doctrine and Covenants 84:54–55).
  • Neglecting daily duties. Wilford Woodruff, quoting Joseph Smith: "The moment you permit yourselves to lay aside any duty that God calls you to perform to gratify your own desires... you lay a foundation for apostasy" (Journal of Discourses 21:190). The duties named are prayer, the sacrament, tithing, meetings, and the Word of Wisdom.
  • Pride and costly apparel. The Nephite collapse began with "those who were lifted up in pride, such as the wearing of costly apparel, and all manner of fine pearls, and of the fine things of the world" (4 Nephi 1:24–25; also 2 Nephi 28; Helaman 3:33–36; Mormon 8:36–39).
  • Murmuring and fault-finding. Heber C. Kimball recorded that Joseph Smith taught "the very step of apostasy commenced with losing confidence in the leaders of this Church and kingdom" (Journal of Discourses 3:227). Joseph Smith again: "That man who rises up to condemn others, finding fault with the Church, saying that they are out of the way, while he himself is righteous, then know assuredly, that that man is in the high road to apostasy" (Forgotten Teachings, p. 71). Thomas B. Marsh lost his standing over a dispute about milk strippings, "saw double," and took a mote for a beam (Journal of Discourses 11:87). The Priesthood Discourses: "The spirit of criticism is the beginning of apostasy" (1975, p. 1).
  • Trusting men instead of God. To David Whitmer: "you have feared man and have not relied on me for strength as you ought... you have been persuaded by those whom I have not commanded" (Doctrine and Covenants 30:1–2). George Q. Cannon: "Do not, brethren, put your trust in man though he be a Bishop, an apostle, or a president; if you do, they will fail you at some time or place... but if we lean on God, He will never fail us" (Deseret Weekly, March 7, 1891). Truth Magazine warns against following leaders "right or wrong" (1:123; 6:58).
  • Shame under mocking. "After they had tasted of the fruit they were ashamed, because of those that were scoffing at them; and they fell away into forbidden paths and were lost" (1 Nephi 8:28).
  • Unrighteous dominion. Controlling other people grieves the Spirit, and "when it is withdrawn, Amen to the priesthood or the authority of that man" (Doctrine and Covenants 121:37).
  • Breaking confidence. Repeating sacred council or private family matters drives the Spirit away (Priesthood Discourses, 1966, p. 38; 1974, p. 253).
  • Setting up stakes to the Almighty. Accepting only the revelations you like. Joseph Smith taught that people who do this find their light turned to darkness (Forgotten Teachings).
  • Hiding it. A testimony hidden out of fear of man can be taken away (Doctrine and Covenants 60:2–3; section 7 above).

Two teachings that go together. Joseph Smith gave what he called a key that will never rust: "Attend to your prayers, pay an honest tithing, obey the authorities, and follow the records of this Church, and you will never apostatize" (Forgotten Teachings, p. 34), and "if you will stay with the majority of the Twelve Apostles, and the records of the Church, you will never be led astray" (pp. 71, 413). The Priesthood Discourses teach that a testimony is kept by loyalty to the presiding Priesthood head and council, and warn that bearing testimony of the Priesthood and then saying the opposite brings "the agony of hell... The spirit of confusion will take a hold of you" (1968, pp. 98, 266; 1972, p. 18; 1975, p. 143). Truth Magazine and Ogden Kraut describe a testimony as an independent witness that judges all counsel "by the Holy Ghost and written revelation," and call relying on "borrowed light" from any leader "a faithless excuse" (Truth Magazine 1:123; Priesthood and Authority, pp. 30, 304). Both teachings require the same thing. The testimony has to be your own, received from God, and the danger named in every collection is losing the Spirit, whether by murmuring against the anointed or by trusting the arm of flesh instead of revelation.

Leaving neutral ground. Anson Call recorded Joseph Smith's warning to a man named Behunnin who said he would never leave the Church.

Brother Behunnin, you don't know what you would do... Before you joined this Church, you stood on neutral ground. When the gospel was preached good and evil were set before you. You could choose either or neither. There were two opposite masters inviting you to serve them. When you joined this Church, you enlisted to serve God. When you did that, you left the neutral ground, and you never can get back on to it again. Should you forsake the Master you have enlisted to serve, it will be at the instigation of the evil one, and you will follow his dictation and be his servant.

Joseph Smith (Anson Call record), Forgotten Teachings of Joseph Smith, p. 712

How a testimony is kept and strengthened.

  • Pray every day, in private and with your family. Brigham Young: bow before the Lord every morning and dedicate yourself (Forgotten Teachings; Rules of Family Conduct, rule 17, in Truth Magazine 10:116).
  • Feast on the word. "Press forward with a steadfastness in Christ... feasting upon the word of Christ, and endure to the end" (2 Nephi 31:19–20). Nourish the tree so it takes root (Alma 32:41–43).
  • Take the sacrament worthily, "that they do always remember him, that they may have his Spirit to be with them" (Moroni 5:2; Doctrine and Covenants 20:77, 79).
  • Follow the whisperings every day. "Sometimes it might be contrary to your judgment; never mind that, follow its dictates" (Joseph Smith to John Taylor).
  • Fast from time to time, keep the Word of Wisdom, and pay an honest tithing (Doctrine and Covenants 89:18–20; Truth Magazine 14:58).
  • Keep a conscience void of offense before God and man (Doctrine and Covenants 93:1; Ogden Kraut, Personal Letters, p. 8).
  • Bear it. This is the one practice that both keeps a testimony and grows it (section 7).
  • Add to your faith virtue, knowledge, temperance, patience, godliness, brotherly kindness, and charity (2 Peter 1, in Priesthood Discourses, 1966, p. 122; 1968, p. 167).
  • Repent quickly. Thomas B. Marsh came back "in deep humility" and put his ears close to the living oracles (Journal of Discourses 11:87).

Promises and warnings. To the faithful witness the sources promise forgiveness of sins (Doctrine and Covenants 84:61; 62:3), garments clean of the blood of this generation (Testimony of the Three Witnesses), hidden treasures of knowledge, health, and protection (89:18–20), the Lord bound to fight your battles (82:10), the First Comforter and then the Second, which is the personal presence of Christ (Teachings, p. 151; Doctrine and Covenants 88:4–5), and the crown of the celestial kingdom for those who are valiant in the testimony of Jesus (76:50–70).

The Book of Mormon reserves its heaviest language for people who received a witness and then denied it: "it would have been better for you that ye had not known me" (2 Nephi 31:14), and "wo unto him that shall deny the revelations of the Lord" (3 Nephi 29:5–6). The revelations call denying the Holy Ghost after receiving it the unpardonable sin (Alma 39:5–6; Doctrine and Covenants 76:31–38). Joseph Smith: "All other sins are not to be compared to sinning against the Holy Ghost, and proving a traitor to the brethren" (Teachings, p. 156). The Lord's own word on staying in the covenant:

And whoso layeth down his life in my cause, for my name's sake, shall find it again, even life eternal. Therefore, be not afraid of your enemies, for I have decreed in my heart, saith the Lord, that I will prove you in all things, whether you will abide in my covenant, even unto death, that you may be found worthy. For if ye will not abide in my covenant ye are not worthy of me.

Doctrine and Covenants 98:13–15

10. Preparing to bear your testimony

If you have been asked to bear your testimony, in a devotional, in a meeting, or to a friend, this is the counsel of the sources gathered into a practical order. Each item points back to a section above.

Before.

  • Ask yourself honestly which of the pillars you know, and which you believe or hope. Bear witness of what you know, and say "I believe" where that is the truth. Belief is an honest and scriptural place to stand (section 4; Alma 32:27).
  • If you are not sure you have a witness, do the work first. Study it out, pray plainly and specifically, fast, make right anything that stands between you and God or a neighbor, and keep at it past the first day (section 6; Doctrine and Covenants 9:7–8; Moroni 10:4).
  • Be clean. A testimony "burns where it goes" only when it comes from a pure soul. Repent before you stand to speak (sections 8 and 9).
  • Remember the times the Spirit has spoken to you: peace, a burning, light, guilt swept away, a change of heart. Those moments are your testimony's evidence. Ponder them the way Moroni 10:3 says to (sections 2 and 6).
  • Do not write a speech, but do write your testimony down for yourself. Putting it "in a phraseology that's good for us" trains the mind and helps you live it. Then set the paper aside and trust the Spirit for the words "in the very moment" (sections 7 and 8).

While bearing it.

  • Declare, and do not lecture. "I know" of God, of Christ, of Joseph Smith, of the Book of Mormon, of the Priesthood, as many or as few as are truly yours (sections 3 and 8).
  • Keep it short. Five words by the Spirit outweigh an hour without it. "Stand up, speak up, and shut up" (section 8).
  • Use your own voice. Plain and natural, with no borrowed piety and no forced emotion (section 8).
  • Leave out contention, speculation, and private confession (section 8).
  • Expect the witness to come while you speak, and not only before. The elder who said "Joseph is a Prophet" received his permanent witness in that sentence (section 7).

After.

  • Know that it is recorded in heaven, and that in bearing it you were promised forgiveness and a brighter light (section 7; Doctrine and Covenants 62:3; 84:61).
  • Keep the daily practices that built it: prayer, the word, the sacrament, obedience to the whisperings. If you neglect them the testimony fades, and the Lord has said that what is treated lightly is taken (section 9).
  • Bear it again, and outside the meeting. Every warned person warns their neighbor (section 7; Doctrine and Covenants 88:81).

11. The short answer

What it is. A testimony is the witness of the Holy Ghost to your own spirit that God lives, that Jesus is the Christ, that Joseph Smith was called of God, that the Book of Mormon and the revelations are true, and that the Priesthood was restored. It is living revelation to one person, and "the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy." It comes to the mind and the heart, as a burning, as peace, as light, or as a still voice.

How it is built. Begin with whatever desire to believe you have, even a particle. Repent and make things right with other people. Study it out, then ask plainly, in secret, with real intent, and keep asking. Fast, and obey, because "if any man will do his will, he shall know." Hold on through the trial, because the witness comes after it. Then it grows line upon line, brighter and brighter, as you follow its whisperings.

Why it has to be spoken. You covenanted at baptism to stand as a witness at all times, in all things, and in all places. The Lord is displeased with those who hide the talent for fear of man, and he takes it from them. A testimony that is spoken is recorded in heaven, brings forgiveness, warns your neighbor, and grows in the bearing. Some people receive the witness while they are bearing it.

How to bear it. By the Spirit, plainly, in your own voice, and briefly. Declare what you know. Leave out contention, speculation, boasting, and private confession. Close in the name of Jesus Christ.

How to keep it. The practices that built it keep it: daily prayer, the word, the sacrament, obedience, fasting, tithing, and bearing it. It is lost through sin, neglect, pride, murmuring against the anointed, trusting the arm of flesh, shame under mocking, and treating lightly what you received. Heber C. Kimball said that no one will be able to stand on borrowed light. Get a testimony of your own, and be valiant in it.

About the sources

Everything on this page comes from seven collections. Where a book is quoted by a short name above, this is the full one.

  • The scriptures. The Book of Mormon in the 1840 edition, the last one Joseph Smith corrected, and the Inspired Version of the Bible. Chapter and verse numbers follow the modern versification, so any edition will find the passage.
  • The Doctrine and Covenants as printed in Joseph Smith's lifetime, with the uncanonized revelations given through him. Section and verse numbers match the modern numbering.
  • Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith (compiled 1938), Forgotten Teachings of Joseph Smith (a compilation of what his contemporaries recorded him saying, cited by page), and the Lectures on Faith delivered in the School of the Prophets at Kirtland in 1834 and 1835.
  • The Journal of Discourses, twenty-six volumes of sermons by Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball, John Taylor, Orson Pratt, Erastus Snow, George Q. Cannon, Wilford Woodruff, and others, 1854 to 1886. Cited as volume:page.
  • The Priesthood Discourses, sermons delivered from 1960 to 1975 by the presiding Priesthood Council, including Leroy S. Johnson, J. Marion Hammon, Rulon T. Jeffs, Alma A. Timpson, Guy H. Musser, Richard S. Jessop, Carl O. N. Holm, and Joseph Zitting. Cited by year and page.
  • Truth Magazine, twenty-one volumes edited by Joseph W. Musser from 1935 to 1956. It reprints many earlier sermons and journals. Cited as volume:page.
  • The Ogden Kraut Library, his compiled books and pamphlets, cited by title and page.
